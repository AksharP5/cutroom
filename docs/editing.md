# Editing behavior

Cutroom's own editor is the default: a tool row, the viewer, the transport, and a timeline below a draggable split. Review notes and word-based edits share its project history.

Settings keeps a New editor switch as a way back to the embedded HyperFrames editor, which still has what Cutroom's does not yet: the source panel. The switch goes away when it arrives.

## Editor

The tool row holds Select, Trim and Blade, then Draw and Comment, as icons on the left. Hovering one names it and its key after a moment; moving to the next names that one at once. The armed tool is a light tile. On the right are snapping, the viewer zoom, Files, undo and redo, and Export. Files opens the project's folder in the file dialog; choosing a file copies its path, for an agent or another editor. Export opens a dialog to choose the range (the whole timeline, or In to Out), the format, the quality, the size, the frame rate, the file name and the folder. It renders the composition the editor has open into the project's `.cutroom/renders` unless you pick another folder. Inside a scene that is the scene alone, from its own In and Out, named after the project and the scene, such as `Launch intro.mp4`. The formats are MP4 (H.264 with AAC), MOV (ProRes 4444 with alpha and AAC), WebM (VP9 with alpha and Opus) and GIF. MOV, WebM and GIF leave transparent whatever the composition doesn't draw, including the page background, so a title exported as MOV keys over other footage in Resolve or another editor; hide or remove a full-frame background clip first. GIF has no sound, its transparency is on or off per pixel, and it plays at 30 fps at most. Quality (Draft, Standard or High) applies to MP4 and WebM; ProRes and GIF have no tiers. Size is the composition's own, or 1080p or 4K in its orientation: the page is drawn at that size rather than stretched, so a 4K export of a 1080p project has sharp text and graphics, while footage in it is scaled up. 1080p and 4K are offered when the composition has their shape and they are a whole multiple of it, or smaller. The frame rate is the composition's or 24, 25, 30 or 60 fps; In and Out keep their times, so a marked export at another rate holds the frames whose times fall between them. When the editor can encode with an NVIDIA GPU, Encode on the GPU is on for MP4 exports; it applies to exports drawn wholly from their sources, and turning it off keeps them on the CPU. Existing files are kept: the dialog suggests a free name, such as the project name followed by ` 2.mp4`. Changing the format changes a typed name's extension. Progress shows on the button and in the status bar, and Cancel export stops the render and removes the partial file. When it finishes, Open plays the file, and the desktop app can also reveal it in its folder. A failure, such as FFmpeg missing, shows in the dialog. Below the settings, Renders lists the project's past exports, newest first: everything in `.cutroom/renders`, and exports saved to other folders while the file is still the one Cutroom wrote. Each row has Open and, in the desktop app, Reveal. Delete asks once more, then removes the file from disk; it can't be undone. Files Cutroom didn't write, or that changed since, never appear, so they can't be deleted from here. Agents start, follow and cancel the same exports through [MCP](mcp.md#export).

The inspector's Transform section moves a visual clip with Position X/Y offsets in composition pixels, scales it with Scale X/Y, turns it with Rotation and trims its edges with Crop Left, Right, Top and Bottom. Scale and Rotation work about the clip's centre, so 110% is a punch-in and a small scale with a Position offset is a picture-in-picture. The link button between the Scale fields scales both axes together; unlinked, each axis scales alone. Crop is in pixels of the clip's own box, before scale and rotation, and never changes layout. Label dragging follows live; release saves one undo step and Escape restores the picture. A typed value shows on the picture as soon as you press Enter. Position adds to ordinary GSAP x/y motion, and Scale and Rotation compose with the clip's authored transform. A composition's own animation can override them: a translate animation, scale, rotation or zoom overrides Position, a scale or rotation tween overrides Scale or Rotation, and an animated clip-path, such as a title's Wipe, overrides Crop while it runs. Transform names the overridden properties at the observed timecode for the selected clip and keeps the fields usable. Reset clears the whole section. A clip whose inline scale, rotation or clip-path Cutroom cannot read, such as one written with `var()` or by another editor, shows why in place of those fields; Position still works.

Position, Scale, Rotation, Crop and Opacity animate with keyframes. Each row has a diamond at the end of its label: press it with the playhead over the clip to add a keyframe there with the value shown, or press it on a keyframe to remove that keyframe. The diamond is faint while the row is static, outlined once it has keyframes, and filled when the playhead is on one. On a keyframed row the fields show the value at the playhead, including between keyframes. Changing the value by typing, dragging the label, the arrow keys, or dragging or scaling the clip on the picture saves it as the keyframe at the playhead, adding one if none is there, and draws on the picture while you drag. Double-clicking a keyframed row's label sets its keyframe at the playhead back to the default; Reset transform leaves keyframed rows alone. Removing a row's last keyframe keeps its value as the static value. The timeline marks a clip's keyframes with diamonds along the foot of its body, one per frame whatever it keys. Click one to move the playhead to it, or drag it along the clip to retime the keyframes on that frame as one undo step; Esc during the drag puts it back. A keyframe cannot move onto another of the same property or off the clip. Right-click a diamond to set the ease of the keyframes on its frame (Linear, Ease in, Ease out, Ease in and out, or Hold, which keeps the value until the next keyframe) or to delete them; a held keyframe is drawn square. An ease shapes the motion from its keyframe to the next. The owner's Resolve keys work too: `[` and `]` move the playhead to the previous and next keyframe of the selected clips, or of every clip when none is selected; Ctrl+`[` adds a keyframe at the playhead on every property the selected clip already animates, or, on a clip with none, on Position, Scale, Rotation and Crop, and on Opacity when it is set; Ctrl+`]` adds a held one the same way, and on an existing keyframe either key only changes its ease; Alt+`]` deletes the keyframes under the playhead. Agents use the same [keyframe commands](mcp.md#keyframes).

With Select, the picture is a direct surface. Rest the pointer on a clip and a thin outline shows which one a press would take, with the move cursor over anything a drag would move. Click a clip where it is drawn to select it. The topmost clip under the pointer wins, Alt-click steps down through the ones beneath it, and Ctrl-click adds or removes one. A click where nothing is drawn, or on the stage around the picture, selects nothing. The selected clip shows a one-pixel outline around its box that follows it through playback and seeks, past the edge of the picture if the clip goes there. Drag the clip to move it: the picture and the inspector's Position follow live in whole pixels, release saves one undo step, and Esc puts it back. Type a Position to place a clip on a fraction of a pixel. Shift keeps the drag to one axis. With snapping on, the box's edges and centre snap to the frame's edges and centre lines within 8px, and a line marks each. Hold Ctrl to drag freely. A click that only selects leaves the arrow keys with the playhead. Click the selected clip again, or drag it, and corner handles appear: the arrows now move it by 1 px, or 10 px with Shift, and a held key is one undo step. Drag a corner handle to scale the clip about its centre. Scale X and Y follow live and keep the clip's proportions; hold Shift to scale each axis alone. Release saves one undo step and Esc puts it back. A press anywhere else removes the handles and returns the arrows to the playhead. Draw and Comment keep the picture to themselves while armed. Clips on locked tracks cannot be picked or moved.

A text clip has a Text section above Transform. Double-click the clip on the picture to open the inspector on that field with the words selected, ready to type over. Enter or leaving the field saves the words as one undo step, Shift+Enter starts a new line, and Esc discards the typing. Line breaks are written as `<br>`; other whitespace collapses as HTML renders it by default. The field is shown but disabled, with the reason under it, when the text has markup inside it, keeps its exact spacing (`<pre>` or an inline `white-space: pre`), or a script writes it by id. Shapes and media have no Text section.

Every visual clip that is not a Cutroom title has a Style section below Composite. It starts closed; once opened it stays open as the selection changes. A clip with words starts it with Font, Weight, Size, Tracking, Leading, Colour, Align and Case; then every clip has Box (width and height), Fill (a colour or a two-colour gradient), Radius, Shadow and Blur, and a video or image adds Fit. Each row starts at Auto, meaning the composition's own stylesheet decides, so a headline an agent wrote keeps its look until you change a row. Setting a row writes that one property inline on the clip and is one undo step; typing `auto`, the row's restore button or a double-click on its label hands it back. Tracking and Leading are percentages of the font size, Font lists the families the project declares, and Shadow follows what the clip draws (its letters, its rounded box, an image's transparency). The head's Reset style button clears every row in one step. Copy style and Paste style in the clip menu carry one clip's style to others; rows a clip cannot take, such as text style on a shape, are left out. The composition's own animation still wins over a row it animates, as it does over Opacity.

The viewer fits the composition inside the stage with a 12px margin, on whole pixels. Fit and 100% are in the zoom menu. The picture outline turns yellow while Draw or Comment is armed.

Ctrl+Alt+G, or Save frame as PNG in the Export dialog, saves the frame at the playhead as a PNG at the composition's size. It goes beside the exports in `.cutroom/renders`, named after the project and the timecode, such as `Launch 00-00-03-12.png`, with a number added when that name is taken. A toast names the file, and the desktop app can reveal it.

Notes are made on the picture. Comment drops a numbered pin where you click and opens a note beside it; Enter adds it, Shift+Enter starts a new line, and Esc discards it. Draw keeps every stroke on the picture until the note is added, and Ctrl+Z takes back the last stroke while the note is open, never the project's last edit. A drawing needs no words. Set end ends a note at the playhead, so it covers a range. Clicking the picture again before adding a note moves its pin and keeps what you typed and drew. The tool stays armed, so the next click starts the next note while the last one saves. A note that fails to save reopens with the reason. Saved notes keep their pins and strokes on the picture while the playhead is on their frame or range, and clicking a pin shows the note in Review. Pins on the picture, dots on the ruler, and rows in Review share one number per note.

The transport follows DaVinci Resolve: L and J play, faster on each press up to 8x; K stops; arrows step frames, seconds, and edits. [Every key](keyboard.md). Timecode is `HH:MM:SS:FF` at the nominal rate, without drop frames. Click it to type a time: digits fill from the right, Enter seeks, and Esc cancels. Playback at 1x is audible, and Mute silences it. Faster speeds and reverse step through frames silently.

I and O set the timeline's in and out points through the same `setRange` command MCP clients use, so each mark is one undo step and agents see it. With loop on, playback repeats the range, or the whole timeline when no range is set.

The viewer also shows one media file at a time as a source, opened from Media. A video shows its picture, an audio file its waveform, and an image a five-second still. The transport bar and its keys then drive the source, a scrub track under it shows its playhead and marked range, and the header above it shows how much would be placed. Insert and Overwrite, or dragging the source onto a track, put the marked part on the timeline through the same `insert` command as a Media drop, with `clip.mediaStart` at the in mark, so it is one undo step. The source is counted in frames at the timeline's rate, so a mark is a whole number of timeline frames. One viewer shows either the source or the timeline, not both side by side: at 1280 and 1720 the space between Media and the inspector fits one picture at a useful size, and the timeline below already shows where an edit lands. [Keys](keyboard.md).

When anything the preview uses changes on disk (HTML, CSS, scripts, or media), whether from an agent, an MCP edit, or another editor, the viewer keeps showing its last frame while the new version loads, then shows the new version at the same frame. If the new version does not load, the last frame stays and the status bar says why.

The status bar also counts HyperFrames lint errors and warnings, checked when the project opens and again after each source change. Click the count to list the findings, errors first, with any fix hint on hover. Choosing one whose element is a clip on the timeline selects that clip and moves the playhead to its start; any other finding copies its file and line. Agents read the same findings with `project_lint`.

### Compositions

Double-click a sub-composition's clip on the timeline, or select it and press Enter, to edit that composition in the same editor. The viewer shows it on its own, and a line above the ruler says where you are: the project, any composition you came through, and the open one. Click a name to go back to it, or press Esc with nothing selected to go up one level. Each composition keeps its own playhead, selection and timeline zoom, so going back finds the root as you left it. The first time a composition opens with the playhead over its clip, its playhead starts at the frame the root was showing.

Editing keys, the inspector, undo and redo, review notes and the agent's context all follow the open composition. A note made inside belongs to it, and opening that note from Review opens the composition first. The footage pipeline setting only draws the root; inside a sub-composition HyperFrames plays everything.

Once a project has a sub-composition, the Media panel's header also offers Compositions: every composition file, the root first, with a picture from a third of the way in, its length, and a count of its HyperFrames lint warnings and errors (red when any is an error; the count's tooltip lists them). The open one has a paper tick on its left edge. Double-click a row, press Enter on it, or choose Open in its menu to edit it. Drag a row onto a track, or choose Insert at playhead or Overwrite at playhead, to place it as a scene clip on the video destination, through the same `insert` command an agent sends, so it is one undo step. The root, and the composition already open, cannot be placed. A placed scene plays its composition from the start, written as the documented host element; the server refuses a scene that would end up playing itself.

### Timeline

The timeline reads the same projection MCP clients get from `timeline_read`, so an agent's edit or an undo shows up as soon as the file changes. Video tracks stack upward from the divider and audio tracks downward; each header has the track's id and name, a lock, and auto-select, both saved through `setTrack` as undoable edits. At rest a header shows those, an audio track's gain, and any switch that is not as a new track has it (disabled, muted, solo, armed); the rest of its switches and the microphone fade in, in place, while the pointer is on the header or focus is inside it. A switch whose setting changes lights for a moment, so a toggle from the header's menu shows even when its button then hides. An audio header also has the track's gain, which plays on top of each clip's own. A clip's volume points ride its gain line; Alt-click adds one, and [Keys](keyboard.md) has the rest. An audio header shows FX when the track has effects; click it, or choose Effects... in the header's menu, to open the track's rack. Compact headers offer Set gain... in the header menu. The inspector shows Combined static gain as a read-only clip × track product, including +24 dB from two +12 dB stages. It excludes fades, automation and authored-group clips, and reads Mixed only when the selected clips would show different values. Clips in an authored audio group keep that group and show a warning that track gain does not apply; [how to set it](keyboard.md). Clips on a locked track dim and cannot be selected.

Insert, Overwrite, and a Media file's Insert at playhead and Overwrite at playhead use the destination tracks, whose codes are filled in their headers. Click a track's code or use Alt+1 to Alt+8 for V1 to V8, Ctrl+Alt+1 to Ctrl+Alt+8 for A1 to A8. Click a lit code or press its key again to turn that incoming kind off. Alt+9 toggles video, Ctrl+Alt+9 toggles audio, and Alt+0 toggles both. A kind remembers its chosen track while off. Ctrl+Shift+Up/Down and Ctrl+Alt+Up/Down step to the header above or below. A missing track key shows a short notice.

Picture goes to the enabled video destination; music, voice and a video's sound go to the enabled audio destination. With audio off, placing b-roll preserves the interview sound. With video off, a video file places only its sound. With both off, placement leaves the timeline and playhead alone. F9 still opens time on participating auto-select tracks; turning a destination off skips incoming media of that kind, rather than excluding existing tracks from ripple edits. A locked enabled destination refuses the placement and names the track. With nothing chosen, or after a chosen track is removed, its remembered destination falls back to V1 or A1. Track choices and enable flags are saved per composition in `.cutroom/view.json`, outside undo history. Dragging media still lands where it is dropped.

Tracks stay in place when their last clip is lifted, cut, moved or overwritten, including after reload and undo/redo. Right-click a track header and choose Remove empty track to delete an unlocked empty row. Removing a track is one undo step. A drop, move or overwrite paste that removes a whole clip names it in a short notice with Undo. Media or a paste placed over part of a clip says "Replaced part of" with its name in the same notice. A clip dragged over part of another stays silent, since the drag showed where it landed, and so does a trim.

The ruler is the scrub surface: press or drag it to move the playhead. While a drag or a shuttle moves the playhead, the viewer draws each frame from a small copy of the footage, made in the background once a clip is on the timeline, and the full frame replaces it when the pointer rests. Its step is the smallest of 1, 2, 5 and 10 frames, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 seconds, and 1, 5, 10, 30 and 60 minutes that keeps labels 96px apart. The in and out points show as brackets with a lighter band between them and a line down through the tracks. Markers sit on the ruler's lower edge with their names in the marker's colour; a name shortens, then hides, rather than run under the next marker or bracket, and shows whole while the pointer or keyboard focus is on its marker. Clicking a marker moves the playhead there. A timecode label disappears when it reaches the track headers, so a scrolled ruler never shows a cut-off time. Every timecode in the app keeps the full `HH:MM:SS:FF` form and draws its zeros quietly: the leading groups that are all zero, and on the ruler a `:00` frames group, are dimmer than the digits that carry the time. Review notes sit on its upper edge as dots: yellow while a note waits on the agent, hatched blue while the agent works on it, solid blue when the work is ready to review, and a hollow ring once accepted. Clicking a dot moves the playhead to the note and shows it in Review.

M adds a named marker at the playhead. Double-click a marker, or focus it and press Enter, to change its name and choose grey, green, violet, rose, or sand. Save commits one undo step; Escape cancels. The same popover can delete the marker. Agents can name and colour markers through MCP `timeline_edit` with `setMarker`, and delete them with `removeMarker`.

A clip's name stays at the left edge of the view while any of the clip is on screen. Names show on clips at least 28px wide and durations at 200px. While a trim, ripple trim or roll is held, each resized clip's duration reads the length it would have and stays at its held edge. It hides whole once the held clip is under 200px, so a duration is never cut through.

Video and image clips show a filmstrip: 40px tiles from the clip's first frame, so a trimmed or retimed clip shows what it plays. Zoomed in until a tile covers less than two seconds of source, each tile shows an exact frame: the first is the clip's first frame, and the rest step on from it in even steps (1 s, 0.5 s, down to a sixteenth), each the last step at or before the tile's left edge. Those frames are fetched one by one, and the nearest earlier sprite frame stands in until each arrives. Zoomed out further, every tile shows a frame from a sprite that holds one every two seconds, so a tile can show footage up to two seconds before its left edge. Audio clips have no name strip: a min/max waveform takes the whole lane under the clip's kind rail, with the name over it. Sound right under its own picture, at the same start and length, leaves the name to the picture and shows its own only while pointed at or selected, or once it is slipped or unlinked. A title shows its words on its body, the first line and then the second, in place of a name strip. A video clip that plays sound carries a 12px strip of it along the bottom of its filmstrip. A muted video, or one whose file has no audio stream, shows no strip. Bodies stay flat colour until the media cache answers, and for compositions and other elements. A clip that is turned off is grey and dimmed, filmstrip and waveform included. When the tracks leave part of the timeline empty at the default density, audio tracks share that room, each up to the Tall height; a track you sized keeps its size, and Compact stays compact. The clip under the pointer lightens. A selected clip's name strip turns paper white with dark text, outlined with a bracket at each end.

Clips are moved, resized, ripple trimmed, rolled, slipped, slid and razored with the mouse, gaps are selected and closed, and media is dragged in from the Media panel. [What each tool does](keyboard.md#mouse). A press becomes a drag after 3px, and from then until the release nothing is sent and nothing renders: the clips are held by transforms where the shared engine's `apply` would put them, a ripple's later clips included, and the filmstrips and waveforms are drawn for that timeline. The release sends the drag's commands as one undo step, through the same queue as the keys. Esc, or an agent removing a dragged clip, ends the drag with nothing changed, and the clips travel back. An edge that would overlap its neighbour or run past its source stops at the last frame the engine takes. A move overwrites covered frames on each destination track, with the surviving heads and tails visible during the drag. A move the engine refuses, onto a locked track or the wrong kind of track, is drawn where it is held with a red outline, and releasing it leaves the timeline as it was and shows the engine's reason in the header. A drag's commands name the clips as the user saw them, so like Q and W they are taken back if an agent moved those clips first. Overwrite moves also freeze what occupies each destination range, including an empty range. If an agent moves or inserts a clip there before the drop or a stale-revision retry, the drag is refused with the agent toast and preserves that clip. Linked partners' destination tracks follow the same rule; gaps between selected clips remain available for unrelated edits.

### Media files

Rename and Remove are in a Media file's right-click menu, and in the `media_rename` and `media_remove` MCP tools.

Rename changes the file's name in its folder and points every reference in the project's sources at the new name: clips in any composition, CSS `url()` values, script strings, and transcript records in `cutroom.json`. Each reference keeps the spelling its author used, such as percent-encoding or a relative `../`. The file and its references change in one undo step. The extension stays, because renaming does not convert a file, and a name another file in that folder already has is refused. References resolve from the file that declares them. Agent instructions, tool configuration and hidden files are never rewritten, so a reference to the file from one of those keeps the old name. Saved source In/Out marks follow rename, undo and redo. If undo, redo or an external rename removes the loaded source path, the viewer closes it before it can be placed again.

Remove never deletes. It moves the file into a `trash` folder at the top of the project, as `trash/talk.mp4`, or `trash/talk 2.mp4` when that name is taken. The folder is an ordinary one on Linux, macOS and Windows, in the browser and in the desktop app, so a removed file can also be dragged back by hand, and emptying the folder in a file manager is what frees the disk space. Media and every agent listing leave the folder out. Range-export preparation leaves it out too, while history keeps tracking it. Undo moves the file back and redo moves it to the trash again. Undo is refused once the file has been deleted from the trash, because history keeps no second copy of video or audio.

When clips, CSS backgrounds, or composition scripts use the file, Remove asks first and reports its uses. A picture and its linked sound count once, including clips in other compositions. Shared CSS and script references count conservatively as at least one use per composition. Those clips stay where they were cut. They show no filmstrip or waveform and play nothing, and the status bar says the preview could not load the file, until the file is back. On the timeline such a clip has a red rail and name over a dark body that reads Media offline, whether the file went while the project was open or was already gone when it opened. An agent gets the same question as a refusal that states the count, and repeats the call with `confirmUsed: true`.

Media drops overwrite the covered frames on the target track by default. Video with sound gets linked audio on the overwritten picture's linked audio track, replacing that same interval. A locked partner track or pictures linked to different audio tracks refuse the drop. With no linked partner under the drop, routing falls back to the first unlocked audio track that holds only linked picture sound and is free over the dropped interval, creating one if needed, so the fallback never cuts existing audio. Music and voice tracks are left alone. Silent video leaves existing audio intact. Hold Ctrl+Shift on Windows/Linux or Cmd+Shift on macOS while dropping to insert instead, splitting and pushing auto-select tracks. These media-drop rules follow the [Resolve 21.1 manual, pages 891-893](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21.1_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=891).

Drag media or move an existing picture clip above the top video track to create a video track. Audio below the last audio track creates an audio track. The clip and its new track form one undo step. The bands appear during media drags, or when a clip move reaches an edge. A drop at an incompatible edge refuses with the existing track-kind message; its ghost never silently disappears.

Q and W ripple trim, E razors, X lifts, Shift+X ripple deletes, D turns clips off or on, and M adds a marker. [Which clips each key acts on](keyboard.md). The timeline shows an edit as soon as the key is pressed, using the same `apply` the server runs. The clips it moves slide from their old place over 220ms, or jump straight there with reduced motion. The server's answer then replaces the shown timeline. A key's commands are built once, when it is pressed, from the timeline on screen, naming the clips it acts on and the ids of every piece it cuts. When an agent edited first, the same commands are sent again on the agent's result; if they still apply, as a marker after an agent's marker does, they land. Q, W, E and Ctrl+\ cut at absolute frames, so they are only sent while the clips they name keep the start, length, track and source offset the user saw. If those clips changed, or the commands no longer apply, the edit is taken back, along with any queued key that named one of its pieces, and the header says "An agent changed those clips first. Press it again." If the agent edits yet again before the resend lands, it says "An agent edited first. Press it again." A refusal in the editor, or by the server when nobody else edited, shows the engine's reason in timeline terms (V1, timecode, clip names). MCP clients get the engine's own text.

Zoom runs from the whole timeline to 32px per frame. The slider in the ruler's corner is logarithmic; double-click it to fit. The timeline fits when a project opens and when you ask, with Shift+Z or that double-click. Edits keep the zoom you have: a long clip dropped in, a ripple, a paste, undo, redo and an agent's edit all change the timeline's length and leave the ruler's scale alone. When the timeline grows past the view, scroll or press Shift+Z to see the rest. Insert and Overwrite leave the playhead at the end of what they placed, and the view scrolls there at the same zoom when that is off screen. Zooming with the wheel or a pinch follows the input and holds the frame under the cursor still. The buttons, the slider's track, and the keys ease to the new zoom around the playhead. During playback the view pages when the playhead reaches 90% of the width. [Keys and gestures](keyboard.md).

## Audio effects

The inspector's Audio section has an effects rack for the selected clip, and Effects... in an audio track header's menu opens the same rack for the whole track. Add effect lists HyperFrames' effects by kind: filters (gain, peaking EQ, shelves, high-pass and low-pass), dynamics (compressor, limiter, true-peak limiter, noise gate), saturation and bitcrush, and time effects (pitch shift, delay, chorus, phaser, reverb). Add preset adds one of HyperFrames' presets, such as Clean Voice, Cut Rumble or Natural Room, as its effects in order, each named for its job. Effects run top to bottom. Open an effect to set its controls in its own units; a number outside its range is held at the nearest end. The power button bypasses an effect, which keeps it in the chain, dimmed, and the cross removes it. Every change is one undo step. A track's effects process all of its clips together, after each clip's own effects and before the track's gain; clips in an authored audio group skip them, as they skip track gain. Preview and export play the same effects. De-essing and noise removal are not among HyperFrames' effects. Agents set the same chains with [`setClip.fx` and `setTrack.fx`](mcp.md#audio-effects).

Duck under voice, in an audio clip's menu, lowers that clip, such as a music bed, by 12 dB while the voice speaks: it comes down over a quarter second before each phrase, comes back over 0.6 s after, and stays down through short pauses. With other clips selected too, the clip you right-clicked is the bed and the others are its voices; otherwise every clip with sound over it on another track that is not named like music or effects is a voice. The duck is volume points on the bed's gain line, multiplied into any it had, so they can be dragged afterwards, and it is one undo step. Normalize loudness to -16 LUFS sets each selected clip's gain so it measures -16 LUFS on its own with FFmpeg's EBU R128 meter; gain stops at +12 dB. A finished whole-timeline export shows its mix's loudness and true peak in the dialog, with Normalize mix to -16 LUFS, which sets a Loudness gain on the mix's master bus; the next export reaches -16 LUFS. Agents use [`audio_duck` and `audio_loudness`](mcp.md#ducking-and-loudness).

## Review

Drawings and comments carry a composition path, frame time, selected element, normalized geometry, and note text. Frame captures are saved under `.cutroom/attachments/`; the note content remains in `cutroom.json` so undo restores it. Captured frames can be reopened from the notebook and are attached when new notes are sent to an agent. Clicking a note opens its composition before seeking to its frame.

A stroke or a comment click opens the note and puts the cursor in its text, so typing never reaches the editor's shortcuts. In the new editor, scrubbing with a note open moves the playhead without changing the note's start frame. Draw adds strokes to that open note at its original frame. Comment moves its pin and start frame to the new click, keeping its words and strokes. Set end uses the playhead as the note's last frame; setting or clearing it returns focus to the text. Esc discards the note and keeps the chosen tool armed.

Ctrl+Z undoes typed words, with a new undo step after a pause of a second, before undoing strokes. Ctrl+Shift+Z restores them. Adding a note closes its popover immediately. Frame captures run in parallel in the background, while each project's notes post in the order they were added, so a slower drawing capture cannot swap its number with a later comment.

Each note stays in the queue until an explicit step finishes it:

| Status            | Meaning                               | Next                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New               | Saved, not yet sent                   | Sending any message queues it. Only new notes can be deleted. |
| Queued            | Waiting for the agent                 | The agent claims, completes, blocks, or withdraws it.         |
| Working           | Claimed by the running agent          | Returns to Queued when the run ends, or when you return it.   |
| Needs your answer | The agent asked a question            | Answer in Review, or in chat; the agent saves a chat answer.  |
| Done              | The agent finished, with a short note | Accept it, or send it back to Queued with what to change.     |
| Withdrawn         | Cancelled, with the reason and actor  | Restore returns it to Queued.                                 |
| Accepted          | You approved the result               | Final.                                                        |

You can withdraw any unfinished note; once sent, a note can only be withdrawn, so its reason is always recorded. The agent can withdraw a queued, working, blocked, or done note when your message asks for it. Accept, send back, answer, restore, and return to queue are yours; an outside MCP client can also do them for you, recorded as an outside agent, but Cutroom's own agent cannot judge its own work. Your answers and send-back notes stay on the note through every later status and are repeated to the agent.

Once sent, a note's text and position no longer change; withdraw it and add a new note instead. Status and your added instructions live in `.cutroom/review.json`, outside undo history, together with a copy of each sent note. That copy is the request. If undo, redo, restore, or an edit outside Cutroom removes a sent note or changes its content, the request stays visible as sent and becomes withdrawn with a reason such as "Removed by undo" or "Changed outside Cutroom", credited to whoever did it. Bringing the content back leaves the request withdrawn, so you decide whether to restore it; restoring needs the content to match what was sent. Cutroom checks for these changes, and saves the result, whenever Review is read and around every undo, redo, and restore.

If `.cutroom/review.json` becomes unreadable, Review shows the error, and statuses stop changing and undo, redo, and restore are blocked until it is repaired, so no sent request is lost; transcripts and chat keep working. An unreadable `cutroom.json` does not block undo, because undo is how it gets repaired. Edits an agent makes to `cutroom.json` directly, rather than through Cutroom's tools, are credited to an outside agent. Notes from earlier versions keep their meaning: sent notes become Queued and resolved notes become Accepted. Their old status fields stay in `cutroom.json` but are ignored once the note has an entry in `review.json`.

Editing a note's text preserves its other fields. Pending text stays in place when switching notebook tabs or hiding panels. Agent drafts also survive project switches and reloads. Review frames and transcription controls stay with their owning project; returning to a running transcription resumes its status updates.

## Script

Word times refer to original media. The displayed timeline time is derived from the current media source, including cuts and moved clips.

A direct cut removes the selected words' source intervals, splits the media element into kept pieces, and ripples subsequent clips on that lane. Other lanes keep their timing. Restoring words rebuilds the kept intervals. Both HTML and script metadata change in one history window.

Direct script edits currently require a local, independently timed media element. Nested timed scenes, linked media, independently changed split pieces, and media-specific scripted animation are refused rather than rewritten ambiguously. Use the agent handoff for those edits.

### Captions

**Add captions** writes the transcript's words to `compositions/captions.html`, a HyperFrames sub-composition, and places it as one clip on a new Captions track above the others. The style menu switches between Karaoke, Word highlight and Plain without changing timing. **Edit caption** on a selected word changes only how captions show it. **SRT** and **VTT** download the lines in composition time. Each of these is one undo step, and agents do the same with `captions_edit` and `captions_export` ([details](mcp.md#captions)).

Captions follow where the words play now, not the raw recording. A script cut or restore rewrites them in the same undo step. A razor, ripple delete, move or trim of the footage on the timeline leaves them as they were and shows **Update captions**, which rewrites them and keeps word corrections and the style. Updating replaces hand edits to the captions file.

The preview and export run the same captions file through the same runtime, with no script, so a frame looks the same in both.

## Timeline commands

Cutroom never rewrites animation scripts when editing the timeline. Root GSAP tween positions are absolute composition seconds. HyperFrames does not add a clip's `data-start`: a root tween at 4 seconds still plays at 4 seconds after its clip moves to 6.

An edit that changes a clip's animation origin can leave its animation behind. This includes moves, nudges, Inspector Start edits, ripples that both trim and displace a clip, and MCP batches. Origin is start minus source in-point at playback rate. Pure head and tail trims that preserve that origin do not trigger feedback. New razor pieces and pasted copies can also lose the original target relationship; Cutroom does not duplicate or retarget their root tweens.

After saving the edit, Cutroom checks for root-script tweens on affected clips. It matches complete literal selectors; loose source hints are reserved for unresolved GSAP target expressions. Unrelated DOM lookups and non-GSAP methods do not count. Sub-composition hosts never warn. When animation is attributable, the initiating editor tab shows one transient toast per edit, naming the clip or saying "3 clips left their animation behind." **Ask agent** saves a Review note asking for a clip-local sub-composition. **Dismiss** closes the toast; otherwise it expires after the existing four-second hold. Leaving the project closes the toast and its action. Observer tabs stay quiet; MCP edits warn their caller through a follow-up notification. Reloading does not bring it back; no warning state is stored. Detection reads source scripts and selectors, without running an animation audit. It can produce an extra nudge for ambiguous scripts and can miss animation whose target cannot be attributed, including some helpers and loops.

Prefer [file-based sub-composition titles](mcp.md#animated-titles). Their timeline is local to the host clip, so animation follows moves, ripples and copies without script edits. CSS transitions and CSS animations on the same properties are outside this feedback and can make a frame depend on seek history even before an edit.

The timeline is a projection of composition HTML in integer frames: a clip starts on the first frame the runtime shows it, and ends after the last. Tracks are `data-track-index` values. Track kind (video or audio), name, lock, and auto-select, plus markers and the in/out range, live in `cutroom.json`; a track keeps its kind when it is empty. Audio clips go on audio tracks and everything else on video tracks; a move, insert, or kind change that breaks this fails with `track_kind`. Commands follow DaVinci Resolve's Edit page:

- Linked clips (`data-link`) are one unit: they move, split, and delete together, unless a `move` says `linked: false`, which moves only the clips it names and leaves them linked, and a trim moves the same edge of every partner by the same number of frames, as with Resolve's Linked Selection. J and L cuts keep their offset.
- `move` defaults to `mode: "overwrite"`, leaving a gap at the source and replacing only the destination frames on each moving clip's track. A covered clip keeps its surviving head or tail; a spanning clip splits, and a fully covered clip disappears. All moving clips are protected from one another. Linked partners move by the same number of frames and overwrite their own tracks, including auto-select-off tracks. `trackBy` changes only named clips' tracks, by track index. A move never creates a track: a destination index with no track is refused with `not_found`, so a batch that wants a new track adds it with `setTrack` (index and kind) before the move, as the editor does when a clip is dragged past the last track. The edit sentence names the destination, such as "Moved 2 tracks up to V3"; optional `trackClipIds` limits that track change to a subset of `clipIds`, so a mixed picture/audio selection moves atomically while only picture changes tracks. Cutroom keeps linked partners on their current tracks, matching Resolve with Linked Move Across Tracks disabled. `mode: "place"` retains overlap refusal.
- Lift (`remove`) leaves a gap. Ripple delete removes the union of the selected clips' time ranges, including linked partners, on the edited tracks and every unlocked auto-select track. Choosing the union for uneven linked selections is Cutroom's policy, not a claim about Resolve. Deleting a J-cut pair can therefore also trim the preceding shot where its picture overlaps the selected audio head. Overlapping ranges close once; separate ranges close from right to left without deleting the time between them. A J-cut head or L-cut tail under a deleted clip loses the overlapping time, so later material closes the full gap.
- A shortening `trim` with `ripple` keeps the clip's start and removes the trimmed time from the other participating tracks. Q removes `[old start, playhead)`; W removes `[playhead, old end)`. Linked partners trim their own corresponding edge by the same number of frames, preserving J/L offsets. Their tracks use that partner's removed range; other tracks use the named clip's range. An extending ripple trim cuts other clips at the old edge and opens exactly the added time there, leaving their source offsets intact.
- Removing `[from, to)` is an explicit cut, never a clamp. Clips ending at or before `from` stay unchanged. Clips starting at or after `to` move left by `to - from`. Clips wholly inside disappear. A clip ending inside keeps its head up to `from`; one starting inside keeps its tail from `to`, moved to `from`, with its source offset advanced. A spanning video or audio clip becomes two adjacent pieces at `from`, with the right piece's source offset advanced to its original frame at `to`. Playback rate applies to every source offset adjustment. A single surviving head or tail keeps its id, including references from scripts and styles. Text, stills and sub-composition hosts spanning the entire removed interval shorten in place, keeping their id, start, source offset, attribution and links to both surviving media pieces. A sub-composition's HTML path is not a video or audio source. The documented title recipe therefore keeps one entrance and one exit; an explicit razor still cuts it. Only a media clip spanning the entire range needs a copied right piece, which takes the next name in the command's `ids` for that clip, or a new random id.
- Locked tracks never change. An auto-select-off track still ripples when it contains an explicitly edited clip or its linked partner; other auto-select-off tracks stay whole. A protected clip may remain linked to a shortened partner when their surviving shared frames retain the same source alignment. For example, a protected picture `[0,100)` stays unchanged while its audio tail `[0,120)` shortens to `[0,100)` after deleting B-roll `[100,150)`. If a protected clip would change or lose sync with surviving partner content, the entire edit fails with `unsupported`, naming that clip. Directly editing an unlinked locked clip still fails with `locked`. Source limits, clip lengths and overlaps must pass exactly; a ripple never reduces the requested edit to make it fit.
- A cut keeps a piece linked while it still overlaps a partner in time; a piece left with no overlapping partner is unlinked.
- `closeGap` closes the empty time around `at` on a track, from the previous clip's end (or the timeline's start) to the next clip's start, on that track and every unlocked auto-select track, as Delete does on a selected gap in Resolve. A clip on one of those tracks inside the gap's time is refused with `unsupported`, naming it, rather than cut; turning off auto-select on its track closes the gap without it. A clip whose linked partner is on a track that does not ripple is refused the same way, since closing the gap would put them out of sync. The space after a track's last clip is not a gap and is refused with `out_of_bounds`, as is a frame inside a clip, and a locked track with `locked`.
- `slip` moves a clip's source in and out by `by` frames in the same place and length: positive shows later source. Linked partners with a source slip by the same frames; `linked: false` slips only the named clip, which puts it out of sync. Video, audio and sub-composition hosts slip; stills and text are refused with `unsupported`. A slip past either end of the source is refused with `out_of_bounds`, saying how far it can go; the editor's drag and keys stop there instead, as Resolve's do. Keyframes, fades and volume points stay on the clip's frames.
- `slide` moves a clip by `by` frames between its neighbours while keeping its source in and out: the clip ending where it starts changes its end and the clip starting where it ends changes its start, each with its source offset adjusted, so no other clip moves. Without a neighbour on a side, the gap there shrinks or grows. Each linked partner slides by the same frames between its own neighbours. A neighbour running out of source, one left shorter than a frame, or the clip reaching the next clip or the timeline's start is refused with `out_of_bounds`, naming the limit and how far it can go. Transitions on its cuts stay, shortened if the media no longer covers them.
- `link` puts the named clips and their existing partners into one new link group; `unlink` takes the named clips out of theirs, and a partner left alone is unlinked too. Neither changes timing. A linked picture and sound of the same file at the same speed that play different source frames at the same time are out of sync: `timeline_read` and edit replies give each a `syncOffset`, frames it plays its source later than its partner, and the timeline shows it as a badge. Moving the clip by `-syncOffset` or slipping it by `syncOffset` brings it back.
- `split` without clip IDs cuts every clip under the frame on auto-select tracks. Right halves of a linked group share a new link. Source-restricted clips and their linked partners stay whole during auto-select razor; explicit razor targets refuse unsupported cuts. Ripple edits instead refuse the whole batch if a participating clip cannot be cut or removed exactly, naming the clip and track. Turn off auto-select on that track to ripple without changing it. This keeps spanning titles and voiceovers aligned with picture unless you explicitly exclude their track.
- A clip id names one clip for the life of the project, across undo and redo. Every id a project has used is listed in `.cutroom/ids.json`, outside undo history, and no new clip takes one of them. `split`, `insert`, overwrite `move`, and ripple `trim` and `remove` take `ids`: for each clip they cut, a list of names for its new pieces in the order the command cuts them; an overwrite move or ripple through several ranges cuts a spanning clip once per range, last range first within each track. A name that is in the timeline or in that list is refused with `duplicate_id`. Pieces without a name, inserted media without `clip.id`, and script cuts get the source id or file name plus six random letters or digits (`talk-k7q2xa`, `talk-cut-m2x9pd`).
- `insert` places a clip, overwrites the range on its own track and its newly linked audio destination when the inserted video has sound, or splits and pushes auto-select tracks. `linkedAudio.track` names the audio track for that sound, which is then edited as the picture's track is; without it the engine routes the sound as a drop does. Inserted video and images fill the composition with their aspect ratio preserved and unused space letterboxed. They are positioned so higher video tracks layer above lower ones. An inserted video is written with `data-has-audio` from its file: `true` when ffprobe finds an audio stream, `false` when it finds none. Each clip in the projection carries `hasAudio`: audio clips, and video that is not `muted` and not marked `data-has-audio="false"`.
- `setClip` with `enabled: false` writes `data-hidden`, which HyperFrames leaves out of preview and render. The clip keeps its place on the timeline.
- `setClip.opacity` sets static inline opacity from 0 to 1, at six-decimal precision. `null` removes the override. Source styles and animations otherwise remain in control.
- `setClip.style` restyles any visual clip that is not a title: text style on text elements, fill, radius, shadow, blur, layout size, and fit on video and images. Each field is one inline declaration in the clip's `style`; the others stay as written, and `null` removes one. [Style semantics](mcp.md#style).
- `setClip.x` and `setClip.y` set additive offsets in composition pixels, with positive X moving right and positive Y moving down. Null or zero clears an axis. These edit only the named visual clip, leaving linked audio in place. Preview and export use the same captured native baselines. [Position semantics and runtime limits](mcp.md#timeline).
- `setClip.scale`, `scaleX`, `scaleY`, `rotation` and `crop` set a visual clip's inline `scale`, `rotate` and `clip-path: inset()`, leaving every other declaration in its `style` as written. 100%, 0° and an empty crop remove the declaration. [Transform semantics](mcp.md#timeline).
- `setKeyframe`, `removeKeyframe` and `moveKeyframe` animate a visual clip's Position, Scale, Rotation, Crop or Opacity with keyframes at clip-local frames. A keyframed property has no static value, and `setClip` refuses it. Trims, ripples and razors keep each keyframe on its frame of content. [Keyframe semantics](mcp.md#keyframes).
- `setClip.text` replaces a text element's words. Newlines become `<br>`, the words are HTML-escaped, and the whitespace around them in the source stays. Media, elements without text, text with markup inside it, preformatted text, and text a script writes by id are refused. [Text semantics](mcp.md#timeline).
- `insert` with `clip.kind: "title"` adds a title, and `setClip.title` changes its look and motion. [Titles](#titles) describes how they follow edits.
- `insert` with `clip.kind: "composition"` places another composition file of the project as a scene: a host element with that file's `data-composition-id`, `data-composition-src` as written from the edited composition's folder, and the file's declared size. It plays from the composition's start. A file that is missing, has no `data-composition-id`, or plays the edited composition through its own scenes is refused, with `not_found` or `unsupported`.
- `setTiming` changes media `playbackRate` or `mediaStart` in seconds, together with linked partners. Duration stays fixed. Source-in shifts partners by the same source offset; speed preserves source sync at the selected clip's start, including split edits. Locked tracks, non-media clips, and insufficient source media are refused.

### Titles

A title is a text clip with a preset entrance and exit: Fade, Slide, Scale, Wipe, or None for each. Its words, font, size, weight, colour, alignment, place on the picture, optional plate behind the words, optional shadow and stroke, and the two motions are settings on the clip, so they are edited as data and never by rewriting a script. Agents add and change titles through [`timeline_edit`](mcp.md#titles). Alt+Shift+T, or Add title in a clip's, lane's or track header's menu, adds a five-second lower-left title at the playhead on a free video track above the video destination and opens the inspector's Text field on its words; [Keys](keyboard.md) has the track rule. The words are editable in the inspector's Text section and by double-clicking the title on the picture, and the title can be dragged on the picture like any clip. A title goes by its first line of words on the timeline, in the inspector and in history, and the name follows when the words change; its element id stays the same.

Select one title and the inspector shows a Title section between Text and Transform: Font, Weight, Size, Colour, Align, Position, Plate, Shadow, Stroke, In and Out. Every change is one undo step and shows on the picture as soon as it saves.

- Font lists the families the project declares with `@font-face`, plus Default, which uses the composition's own font. New projects declare Instrument Sans, Oswald, Fraunces and Martian Mono. To add another, put its file in the project and declare it with `@font-face`.
- Size is in composition pixels; drag its label or use Up and Down, as with Position.
- Colour is a swatch that opens the system colour picker, with the same colour as six hex digits beside it. Moving through colours in the picker saves once, when the picker closes.
- Position picks one of five places on the picture and clears any Position X/Y offset in the same undo step, so the title lands on the place you chose.
- Plate puts a filled box behind the words. Ticking it starts from black at 70%; the swatch, hex and percentage beside it set its colour and opacity.
- Shadow and Stroke help words read over busy footage without a plate. Shadow drops a soft copy of the words just below them; ticking it starts from black at 70% with a 12 px blur, and the swatch, hex, percentage and Blur beside and below it change those. Stroke draws an outline around the letters, outside their shape so they keep their weight; it starts black and 3 px thick, with its colour and Width beside and below it. Sizes are in composition pixels.
- In and Out each take a preset and a length, typed as timecode, frames (`12f`) or seconds (`0.4s`). The length is disabled while the preset is None.

To give other titles the same look, right-click a title and choose Copy style, then select the others and choose Paste style. It copies every Title setting, motions and Position included, but not the words, timing or Transform settings, and pasting onto several titles is one undo step. The copied style lasts until the editor reloads and belongs to its project, because fonts do.

The section edits one title at a time. Several selected clips show the clip count, as for every other inspector section. A title Cutroom cannot read shows the reason in the section in place of the controls.

The entrance belongs to the clip's first frame and the exit to its last:

- Trimming the tail moves the exit to the new last frame. Trimming the head plays the whole entrance from the new first frame, like a fade handle, where video would start partway into its source.
- Moves, ripples, copies and pastes carry both with the clip.
- Removing time through the middle of a title shortens it in place, so it still enters once and exits once.
- A razor cut gives the first piece the entrance and the second piece the exit. Neither animates at the cut, so the words hold across it. Each piece's missing motion reads None and can be set again.
- When the entrance and exit together are longer than the clip, both shorten in proportion, as audio fades do.

A title is one element in the composition's HTML plus one stylesheet block Cutroom keeps current, with no script. Preview, `timeline_view` and export read the same attributes through the same HyperFrames runtime, so they agree. Two limits follow from how it is built:

- The stylesheet uses typed CSS `attr()`, which needs Chromium 133 or newer. The desktop app, frame capture and export all qualify. If you open the editor in Firefox or Safari, titles show unstyled and unanimated in the viewer; the exported video is still correct.
- A title can only use a font the project declares with an uncommented `@font-face` rule. A system font can resolve to a different face in export than in the viewer.

If a source edit gives a title an undeclared font, invalid settings, markup other than `<br>`, or empty words, the timeline reads it as an ordinary element with `title: null` and a reason in `sourceRestrictions.title`. Repair its source before changing title settings.

Titles an agent wrote as sub-composition files or root scripts are left as they are and keep the behavior described above.

### Transitions

A cross dissolve or a dip to black sits on a cut between two video, image or audio clips, and their linked sound crossfades with it. It plays the media past each clip's edit point, as Resolve does: centred on the cut, the outgoing clip runs on for half its length and the incoming clip starts half its length early. A transition that needs more media than the clips have is refused with the longest that fits. Ctrl+T adds a one-second cross dissolve at the nearest cut, Alt+T one for picture alone and Shift+T one for sound alone; [Keys](keyboard.md) has the rule for which cut. On the timeline a transition is a pale box with a diagonal over its cut, its length printed inside once it is 28px wide, so a mixed cut reads apart from a hard cut. Select a transition by clicking it on the timeline to change its kind, length and alignment in the inspector, drag its ends to change its length, or press Delete to remove it. Agents set them with [`setTransition`](mcp.md#transitions).

- A roll moves the transition with the cut. A trim that leaves less room shortens it.
- A razor elsewhere in either clip keeps it on the piece that still meets the cut, and adds none at the razor.
- Moving either clip away, a trim that opens a gap, or deleting either clip removes it.
- Its edges own their fades: change the transition instead.
- Sound crossfades on equal-power curves, so related and unrelated sound both hold their level through the cut.

In the source, each clip's element runs past the cut by its share, with `data-transition-*` attributes, and one stylesheet block Cutroom keeps current ramps the picture. Like titles, it needs Chromium 133 or newer in the viewer; the export is correct regardless.

These cuts follow Resolve's sync-preserving Edit-page ripple model introduced in [DaVinci Resolve 20.2, New Features Guide, pages 7-8](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/SupportNotes/DaVinci_Resolve_20.2_New_Features_Guide.pdf#page=7). Earlier Resolve versions mainly moved clips starting after the edit, leaving spanning music or titles uncut. Resolve 20.2 also added independent Sync Lock controls, explained on page 12. Cutroom currently uses auto-select for ripple participation and refuses linked edits across excluded tracks; it does not expose Resolve's separate Sync Lock toggle or its preference to restore the older ripple behavior.

The [Resolve 21 Reference Manual](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf) describes ripple delete in "Deleting Clips From the Timeline", page 933, the shared ripple rules and cuts in "Ripple Editing Rules", page 1100 and "Sync Lock Controls" on page 1101, and the playhead trim commands in "Trim Start and Trim End" and "Resize, Ripple, and Roll Start and End Commands", pages 1115-1117. Q and W are the owner's bindings, not Resolve's factory shortcuts. The interval cases above make the documented before/after sync rule deterministic for Cutroom; the manuals do not enumerate each partial-overlap case or document a linked smallest-gap exception.

Resolve's Selection-mode drag overwrite is documented in the [Resolve 21 Reference Manual, page 964](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=964). The [linked-selection rules on pages 936-937](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=936) move selected partners together. Each partner therefore overwrites on its own destination track; the page 964 example shows both picture and sound doing so. Partial coverage trims away the covered frames, complete coverage removes the clip, and coverage inside a clip leaves two pieces. The manual illustrates the interior split and partial trim explicitly on [pages 742-743](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=742), in its Cut-page overwrite examples.

The alternative is a shuffle/swap insert, documented on [pages 969-970](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=969): Cmd+Shift drag on macOS, Ctrl+Shift on Windows/Linux per the [platform conventions on page 56](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=56). This closes the source gap and moves surrounding clips around the selection, using the grabbed track's span for split edits. It is not simply an insert at the destination. Cutroom does not yet implement shuffle moves; Ctrl/Cmd during a drag continues to bypass snapping.

A command that cannot apply exactly fails with a typed error such as `overlap`, `locked`, or `out_of_bounds`; values are never clamped. Overwrite moves clear covered frames before checking for overlaps; `place` moves refuse them. Overlaps already authored in HTML stay editable. Video and audio without `data-duration` take their length from the source file, measured with ffprobe; trims cannot run past the end of the source. A start written as a clip reference (`data-start="intro"`) is kept while it still resolves to the edited position and becomes an absolute time otherwise. Clips styled or animated by id, or containing elements with ids, cannot be split because the copy would lose or duplicate them. The root's length keeps its authored tail after the last clip; a root kept shorter than its content does not grow, and clips placed in an empty composition only lengthen it if they run past its end.

A batch writes only the attributes it changes, removed elements, and new clips; all other bytes stay as authored. Clips without an `id` get their positional id written when an edit touches them or would renumber them. It is one undo step, and a stale revision is refused.

## Concurrent edits and undo

Editor writes, script changes, annotations, and MCP commands share a history. A save based on an older source version returns a conflict instead of overwriting a newer edit. External agents can also edit files directly; the watcher refreshes the preview and the history observes those changes. Accepted outside changes update a clean source buffer even while it has focus. Pending typing keeps the normal save and conflict handling.

Undo can conflict with a newer outside edit. Resolve that conflict before applying another change. Original video and audio files remain intact.
