# Editing behavior

Cutroom's own editor is the default: a tool row, the viewer, the transport, and a timeline below a draggable split. Review notes and word-based edits share its project history.

Settings keeps a New editor switch as a way back to the embedded HyperFrames editor, which still has what Cutroom's does not yet: the inspector, the composition and source panels, frame capture, and export settings with a cancel button. The switch goes away when those arrive.

## Editor

The tool row holds Select, Trim, Blade, Draw and Comment on the left. On the right are snapping, the viewer zoom, Files, undo and redo, and Export. Files opens the project's folder in the file dialog; choosing a file copies its path, for an agent or another editor. Export renders the root composition to MP4 at the timeline's frame rate and standard quality, shows its progress on the button and in the status bar, and downloads it as the project name followed by `.mp4` when it is done. A failure, such as FFmpeg missing, shows in the header.

The viewer fits the composition inside the stage with a 12px margin, on whole pixels. Fit and 100% are in the zoom menu. The picture outline turns yellow while Draw or Comment is armed.

Notes are made on the picture. Comment drops a numbered pin where you click and opens a note beside it; Enter adds it, Shift+Enter starts a new line, and Esc discards it. Draw keeps every stroke on the picture until the note is added, and Ctrl+Z takes back the last stroke while the note is open, never the project's last edit. A drawing needs no words. Set end ends a note at the playhead, so it covers a range. Clicking the picture again before adding a note moves its pin and keeps what you typed and drew. The tool stays armed, so the next click starts the next note while the last one saves. A note that fails to save reopens with the reason. Saved notes keep their pins and strokes on the picture while the playhead is on their frame or range, and clicking a pin shows the note in Review. Pins on the picture, dots on the ruler, and rows in Review share one number per note.

The transport follows DaVinci Resolve: L and J play, faster on each press up to 8x; K stops; arrows step frames, seconds, and edits. [Every key](keyboard.md). Timecode is `HH:MM:SS:FF` at the nominal rate, without drop frames. Click it to type a time: digits fill from the right, Enter seeks, and Esc cancels. Playback at 1x is audible, and Mute silences it. Faster speeds and reverse step through frames silently.

I and O set the timeline's in and out points through the same `setRange` command MCP clients use, so each mark is one undo step and agents see it. With loop on, playback repeats the range, or the whole timeline when no range is set.

When anything the preview uses changes on disk (HTML, CSS, scripts, or media), whether from an agent, an MCP edit, or another editor, the viewer keeps showing its last frame while the new version loads, then shows the new version at the same frame. If the new version does not load, the last frame stays and the status bar says why.

### Timeline

The timeline reads the same projection MCP clients get from `timeline_read`, so an agent's edit or an undo shows up as soon as the file changes. Video tracks stack upward from the divider and audio tracks downward; each header has the track's id and name, a lock, and auto-select, both saved through `setTrack` as undoable edits. Clips on a locked track dim and cannot be selected.

The ruler is the scrub surface: press or drag it to move the playhead. Its step is the smallest of 1, 2, 5 and 10 frames, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 seconds, and 1, 5, 10, 30 and 60 minutes that keeps labels 96px apart. The in and out points show as brackets with a lighter band between them and a line down through the tracks. Markers sit on the ruler's lower edge with their names; a name shortens, then hides, rather than run under the next marker or bracket. Clicking a marker moves the playhead there. A timecode label disappears when it reaches the track headers, so a scrolled ruler never shows a cut-off time. Review notes sit on its upper edge as dots: yellow while a note waits on the agent, hatched blue while the agent works on it, solid blue when the work is ready to review, and a hollow ring once accepted. Clicking a dot moves the playhead to the note and shows it in Review.

A clip's name stays at the left edge of the view while any of the clip is on screen. Names show on clips at least 28px wide and durations at 200px.

Video and image clips show a filmstrip: 40px tiles from the clip's first frame, so a trimmed or retimed clip shows what it plays. Zoomed in until a tile covers less than two seconds of source, each tile shows an exact frame: the first is the clip's first frame, and the rest step on from it in even steps (1 s, 0.5 s, down to a sixteenth), each the last step at or before the tile's left edge. Those frames are fetched one by one, and the nearest earlier sprite frame stands in until each arrives. Zoomed out further, every tile shows a frame from a sprite that holds one every two seconds, so a tile can show footage up to two seconds before its left edge. Audio clips show a min/max waveform, and a video clip that plays sound carries a 12px strip of it along the bottom of its filmstrip. A muted video, or one whose file has no audio stream, shows no strip. Bodies stay flat colour until the media cache answers, and for compositions and other elements. A clip that is turned off is grey and dimmed, filmstrip and waveform included.

Clips are moved, resized, ripple trimmed, rolled and razored with the mouse, and media is dragged in from the Media panel. [What each tool does](keyboard.md#mouse). A press becomes a drag after 3px, and from then until the release nothing is sent and nothing renders: the clips are held by transforms where the shared engine's `apply` would put them, a ripple's later clips included, and the filmstrips and waveforms are drawn for that timeline. The release sends the drag's commands as one undo step, through the same queue as the keys. Esc, or an agent removing a dragged clip, ends the drag with nothing changed, and the clips travel back. An edge that would overlap its neighbour or run past its source stops at the last frame the engine takes. A move overwrites covered frames on each destination track, with the surviving heads and tails visible during the drag. A move the engine refuses, onto a locked track or the wrong kind of track, is drawn where it is held with a red outline, and releasing it leaves the timeline as it was and shows the engine's reason in the header. A drag's commands name the clips as the user saw them, so like Q and W they are taken back if an agent moved those clips first. Overwrite moves also freeze what occupies each destination range, including an empty range. If an agent moves or inserts a clip there before the drop or a stale-revision retry, the drag is refused with the agent toast and preserves that clip. Linked partners' destination tracks follow the same rule; gaps between selected clips remain available for unrelated edits.

Q and W ripple trim, E razors, X lifts, Shift+X ripple deletes, D turns clips off or on, and M adds a marker. [Which clips each key acts on](keyboard.md). The timeline shows an edit as soon as the key is pressed, using the same `apply` the server runs. The clips it moves slide from their old place over 220ms, or jump straight there with reduced motion. The server's answer then replaces the shown timeline. A key's commands are built once, when it is pressed, from the timeline on screen, naming the clips it acts on and the ids of every piece it cuts. When an agent edited first, the same commands are sent again on the agent's result; if they still apply, as a marker after an agent's marker does, they land. Q, W, E and Ctrl+\ cut at absolute frames, so they are only sent while the clips they name keep the start, length, track and source offset the user saw. If those clips changed, or the commands no longer apply, the edit is taken back, along with any queued key that named one of its pieces, and the header says "An agent changed those clips first. Press it again." If the agent edits yet again before the resend lands, it says "An agent edited first. Press it again." A refusal in the editor, or by the server when nobody else edited, shows the engine's reason in timeline terms (V1, timecode, clip names). MCP clients get the engine's own text.

Zoom runs from the whole timeline to 32px per frame. The slider in the ruler's corner is logarithmic; double-click it to fit. Zooming with the wheel or a pinch follows the input and holds the frame under the cursor still. The buttons, the slider's track, and the keys ease to the new zoom around the playhead. During playback the view pages when the playhead reaches 90% of the width. [Keys and gestures](keyboard.md).

## Review

Drawings and comments carry a composition path, frame time, selected element, normalized geometry, and note text. Frame captures are saved under `.cutroom/attachments/`; the note content remains in `cutroom.json` so undo restores it. Captured frames can be reopened from the notebook and are attached when new notes are sent to an agent. Clicking a note opens its composition before seeking to its frame.

A stroke or a comment click opens the note and puts the cursor in its text, so typing never reaches the editor's shortcuts. In the new editor, scrubbing with a note open moves the playhead without changing the note's start frame. Draw adds strokes to that open note at its original frame. Comment moves its pin and start frame to the new click, keeping its words and strokes. Set end uses the playhead as the note's last frame; setting or clearing it returns focus to the text. Esc discards the note and keeps the chosen tool armed.

Ctrl+Z undoes typed words, with a new undo step after a pause of a second, before undoing strokes. Ctrl+Shift+Z restores them. Adding a note closes its popover immediately. Frame captures run in parallel in the background, while each project's notes post in the order they were added, so a slower drawing capture cannot swap its number with a later comment.

Each note stays in the queue until an explicit step finishes it:

| Status            | Meaning                               | Next                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New               | Saved, not yet sent                   | Sending any message queues it. Only new notes can be deleted. |
| Queued            | Waiting for the agent                 | The agent claims, completes, blocks, or withdraws it.         |
| Working           | Claimed by the running agent          | Returns to Queued when the run ends, or when you return it.   |
| Needs your answer | The agent asked a question            | Answer in Review, or in chat; the agent saves a chat answer.  |
| Done              | The agent finished, with a short note | Accept it, or send it back to Queued with what to change.     |
| Withdrawn         | Cancelled, with the reason and actor  | Restore returns it to Queued.                                 |
| Accepted          | You approved the result               | Final.                                                        |

You can withdraw any unfinished note; once sent, a note can only be withdrawn, so its reason is always recorded. The agent can withdraw a queued, working, blocked, or done note when your message asks for it. Accept, send back, answer, restore, and return to queue are yours; an outside MCP client can also do them for you, recorded as an outside agent, but Cutroom's own agent cannot judge its own work. Your answers and send-back notes stay on the note through every later status and are repeated to the agent.

Once sent, a note's text and position no longer change; withdraw it and add a new note instead. Status and your added instructions live in `.cutroom/review.json`, outside undo history, together with a copy of each sent note. That copy is the request. If undo, redo, restore, or an edit outside Cutroom removes a sent note or changes its content, the request stays visible as sent and becomes withdrawn with a reason such as "Removed by undo" or "Changed outside Cutroom", credited to whoever did it. Bringing the content back leaves the request withdrawn, so you decide whether to restore it; restoring needs the content to match what was sent. Cutroom checks for these changes, and saves the result, whenever Review is read and around every undo, redo, and restore.

If `.cutroom/review.json` becomes unreadable, Review shows the error, and statuses stop changing and undo, redo, and restore are blocked until it is repaired, so no sent request is lost; transcripts and chat keep working. An unreadable `cutroom.json` does not block undo, because undo is how it gets repaired. Edits an agent makes to `cutroom.json` directly, rather than through Cutroom's tools, are credited to an outside agent. Notes from earlier versions keep their meaning: sent notes become Queued and resolved notes become Accepted. Their old status fields stay in `cutroom.json` but are ignored once the note has an entry in `review.json`.

Editing a note's text preserves its other fields. Pending text stays in place when switching notebook tabs or hiding panels. Agent drafts also survive project switches and reloads. Review frames and transcription controls stay with their owning project; returning to a running transcription resumes its status updates.

## Script

Word times refer to original media. The displayed timeline time is derived from the current media source, including cuts and moved clips.

A direct cut removes the selected words' source intervals, splits the media element into kept pieces, and ripples subsequent clips on that lane. Other lanes keep their timing. Restoring words rebuilds the kept intervals. Both HTML and script metadata change in one history window.

Direct script edits currently require a local, independently timed media element. Nested timed scenes, linked media, independently changed split pieces, and media-specific scripted animation are refused rather than rewritten ambiguously. Use the agent handoff for those edits.

## Timeline commands

The timeline is a projection of composition HTML in integer frames: a clip starts on the first frame the runtime shows it, and ends after the last. Tracks are `data-track-index` values. Track kind (video or audio), name, lock, and auto-select, plus markers and the in/out range, live in `cutroom.json`; a track keeps its kind when it is empty. Audio clips go on audio tracks and everything else on video tracks; a move, insert, or kind change that breaks this fails with `track_kind`. Commands follow DaVinci Resolve's Edit page:

- Linked clips (`data-link`) are one unit: they move, split, and delete together, unless a `move` says `linked: false`, which moves only the clips it names and leaves them linked, and a trim moves the same edge of every partner by the same number of frames, as with Resolve's Linked Selection. J and L cuts keep their offset.
- `move` defaults to `mode: "overwrite"`, leaving a gap at the source and replacing only the destination frames on each moving clip's track. A covered clip keeps its surviving head or tail; a spanning clip splits, and a fully covered clip disappears. All moving clips are protected from one another. Linked partners move by the same number of frames and overwrite their own tracks, including auto-select-off tracks. `trackBy` changes only named clips' tracks; optional `trackClipIds` limits that track change to a subset of `clipIds`, so a mixed picture/audio selection moves atomically while only picture changes tracks. Cutroom keeps linked partners on their current tracks, matching Resolve with Linked Move Across Tracks disabled. `mode: "place"` retains overlap refusal.
- Lift (`remove`) leaves a gap. Ripple delete removes the union of the selected clips' time ranges, including linked partners, on the edited tracks and every unlocked auto-select track. Choosing the union for uneven linked selections is Cutroom's policy, not a claim about Resolve. Deleting a J-cut pair can therefore also trim the preceding shot where its picture overlaps the selected audio head. Overlapping ranges close once; separate ranges close from right to left without deleting the time between them. A J-cut head or L-cut tail under a deleted clip loses the overlapping time, so later material closes the full gap.
- A shortening `trim` with `ripple` keeps the clip's start and removes the trimmed time from the other participating tracks. Q removes `[old start, playhead)`; W removes `[playhead, old end)`. Linked partners trim their own corresponding edge by the same number of frames, preserving J/L offsets. Their tracks use that partner's removed range; other tracks use the named clip's range. An extending ripple trim cuts other clips at the old edge and opens exactly the added time there, leaving their source offsets intact.
- Removing `[from, to)` is an explicit cut, never a clamp. Clips ending at or before `from` stay unchanged. Clips starting at or after `to` move left by `to - from`. Clips wholly inside disappear. A clip ending inside keeps its head up to `from`; one starting inside keeps its tail from `to`, moved to `from`, with its source offset advanced. A spanning clip becomes two adjacent pieces at `from`, with the right piece's source offset advanced to its original frame at `to`. Playback rate applies to every source offset adjustment. A single surviving head or tail keeps its id, including references from scripts and styles. Only a clip spanning the entire range needs a copied right piece, which takes the next name in the command's `ids` for that clip, or a new random id.
- Locked tracks never change. An auto-select-off track still ripples when it contains an explicitly edited clip or its linked partner; other auto-select-off tracks stay whole. A protected clip may remain linked to a shortened partner when their surviving shared frames retain the same source alignment. For example, a protected picture `[0,100)` stays unchanged while its audio tail `[0,120)` shortens to `[0,100)` after deleting B-roll `[100,150)`. If a protected clip would change or lose sync with surviving partner content, the entire edit fails with `unsupported`, naming that clip. Directly editing an unlinked locked clip still fails with `locked`. Source limits, clip lengths and overlaps must pass exactly; a ripple never reduces the requested edit to make it fit.
- A cut keeps a piece linked while it still overlaps a partner in time; a piece left with no overlapping partner is unlinked.
- `split` without clip IDs cuts every clip under the frame on auto-select tracks. Right halves of a linked group share a new link.
- A clip id names one clip for the life of the project, across undo and redo. Every id a project has used is listed in `.cutroom/ids.json`, outside undo history, and no new clip takes one of them. `split`, `insert`, overwrite `move`, and ripple `trim` and `remove` take `ids`: for each clip they cut, a list of names for its new pieces in the order the command cuts them; an overwrite move or ripple through several ranges cuts a spanning clip once per range, last range first within each track. A name that is in the timeline or in that list is refused with `duplicate_id`. Pieces without a name, inserted media without `clip.id`, and script cuts get the source id or file name plus six random letters or digits (`talk-k7q2xa`, `talk-cut-m2x9pd`).
- `insert` places a clip, overwrites the range on its own track (linked partners on other tracks stay whole), or splits and pushes auto-select tracks. Inserted video and images fill the composition with their aspect ratio preserved and unused space letterboxed. They are positioned so higher video tracks layer above lower ones. An inserted video is written with `data-has-audio` from its file: `true` when ffprobe finds an audio stream, `false` when it finds none. Each clip in the projection carries `hasAudio`: audio clips, and video that is not `muted` and not marked `data-has-audio="false"`.
- `setClip` with `enabled: false` writes `data-hidden`, which HyperFrames leaves out of preview and render. The clip keeps its place on the timeline.

These cuts follow Resolve's sync-preserving Edit-page ripple model introduced in [DaVinci Resolve 20.2, New Features Guide, pages 7-8](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/SupportNotes/DaVinci_Resolve_20.2_New_Features_Guide.pdf#page=7). Earlier Resolve versions mainly moved clips starting after the edit, leaving spanning music or titles uncut. Resolve 20.2 also added independent Sync Lock controls, explained on page 12. Cutroom currently uses auto-select for ripple participation and refuses linked edits across excluded tracks; it does not expose Resolve's separate Sync Lock toggle or its preference to restore the older ripple behavior.

The [Resolve 21 Reference Manual](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf) describes ripple delete in "Deleting Clips From the Timeline", page 933, the shared ripple rules and cuts in "Ripple Editing Rules", page 1100 and "Sync Lock Controls" on page 1101, and the playhead trim commands in "Trim Start and Trim End" and "Resize, Ripple, and Roll Start and End Commands", pages 1115-1117. Q and W are the owner's bindings, not Resolve's factory shortcuts. The interval cases above make the documented before/after sync rule deterministic for Cutroom; the manuals do not enumerate each partial-overlap case or document a linked smallest-gap exception.

Resolve's Selection-mode drag overwrite is documented in the [Resolve 21 Reference Manual, page 964](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=964). The [linked-selection rules on pages 936-937](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=936) move selected partners together. Each partner therefore overwrites on its own destination track; the page 964 example shows both picture and sound doing so. Partial coverage trims away the covered frames, complete coverage removes the clip, and coverage inside a clip leaves two pieces. The manual illustrates the interior split and partial trim explicitly on [pages 742-743](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=742), in its Cut-page overwrite examples.

The alternative is a shuffle/swap insert, documented on [pages 969-970](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=969): Cmd+Shift drag on macOS, Ctrl+Shift on Windows/Linux per the [platform conventions on page 56](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=56). This closes the source gap and moves surrounding clips around the selection, using the grabbed track's span for split edits. It is not simply an insert at the destination. Cutroom does not yet implement shuffle moves; Ctrl/Cmd during a drag continues to bypass snapping.

A command that cannot apply exactly fails with a typed error such as `overlap`, `locked`, or `out_of_bounds`; values are never clamped. Overwrite moves clear covered frames before checking for overlaps; `place` moves refuse them. Overlaps already authored in HTML stay editable. Video and audio without `data-duration` take their length from the source file, measured with ffprobe; trims cannot run past the end of the source. A start written as a clip reference (`data-start="intro"`) is kept while it still resolves to the edited position and becomes an absolute time otherwise. Clips styled or animated by id, or containing elements with ids, cannot be split because the copy would lose or duplicate them. The root's length keeps its authored tail after the last clip; a root kept shorter than its content does not grow, and clips placed in an empty composition only lengthen it if they run past its end.

A batch writes only the attributes it changes, removed elements, and new clips; all other bytes stay as authored. Clips without an `id` get their positional id written when an edit touches them or would renumber them. It is one undo step, and a stale revision is refused.

## Concurrent edits and undo

Editor writes, script changes, annotations, and MCP commands share a history. A save based on an older source version returns a conflict instead of overwriting a newer edit. External agents can also edit files directly; the watcher refreshes the preview and the history observes those changes. Accepted outside changes update a clean source buffer even while it has focus. Pending typing keeps the normal save and conflict handling.

Undo can conflict with a newer outside edit. Resolve that conflict before applying another change. Original video and audio files remain intact.
