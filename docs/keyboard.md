# Keyboard

Most editor shortcuts follow the owner's DaVinci Resolve Edit page preset. Shared actions keep their Resolve action ids. Shortcuts never fire while you type in a field or work in a dialog, except the [panel keys](#panels), which leave a field. Space and Enter activate a button, checkbox, switch or menu item reached by keyboard. Mouse clicks on editing and transport buttons release their focus, so Space plays afterward. A mouse click on an inspector checkbox, radio, toggle or colour returns the keyboard to the timeline, so Space, J, K, L and the arrows drive the transport; reached by Tab, those controls keep their own keys. Dialog openers retain focus. Pointer menus return to the field they interrupted, or to the timeline when no field was being edited. Keyboard menus return to their opener. Fields, dialogs and menus keep their own keys. Esc leaves the agent composer or an inline field for the timeline, keeping the composer draft and cancelling an unfinished rename. A right-click on a track name opens its menu without starting a rename. Esc returns to Select. During voiceover recording, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V, Space and K stop and save, and Esc stops and asks before discarding the take, including when a field has focus. Cancelling saves it.

Projects → New project focuses Project name, so typing and Enter create the project immediately. The Edit marker popover keeps Escape while open, including after Tab moves beyond Save; Escape discards the unsaved draft and returns focus to its opener.

Edit-page and global preset keys the editor receives without a matching action show a short "Shortcut not yet supported" notice. Tab moves focus, Shift+F10 opens the context menu, and media keys keep their system behavior.

`,` and `.` nudge the selected clips and their linked partners by one frame, and Shift+`,` and Shift+`.` by five, overwriting what they land on. Each tap is one undo step, and a partner on a locked track stops the nudge with a notice. Hold a nudge, slip, slide, or trim key to repeat on the same clips. The timeline shows each step as you hold it. Those steps save together as one undo entry when you release the key, switch away from the tab, or start another edit. Cutroom warns before leaving the page with an outstanding save. Outside edits can still appear during the hold. With the Trim tool armed they slip the selected clips instead, with their linked partners: the picture moves the way the key points, so `.` shows one frame earlier of the source. S switches them to slide, which moves the selected clips between their neighbours as one block, so clips next to each other move together and only the clips either side trim, and back, as Resolve's Toggle Slip/Slide Mode does; a notice names the mode. Alt+`,` and Alt+`.` slip only the selected sound, which puts it out of sync with its picture, and the badge on both clips shows by how many frames. Ctrl+Alt+L unlinks the selected clips when any of them is linked and links them otherwise, as Resolve's Link Clips does; the clip menu shows it checked while they are linked. With a gap selected, X, Backspace, Shift+X and Delete all close it. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the selected clips to the next track of their kind as the headers stack them; a clip with no track there stays, and a move that would cover another clip is refused. F opens the media under the playhead in the source viewer at the same frame: the selected clip there, or else the top picture, then the first sound, on auto-select tracks. Ctrl+D and R open the inspector on the selected clip with Duration or Speed ready to type. Shift+Up and Shift+Down go to the previous and next timeline marker.

V selects the nearest edit point on the enabled video destination, or the enabled audio destination if the video destination has no edit point. Locked tracks are skipped. Brackets show the selected edges, including linked partners. At a cut, V selects both sides for a roll. U cycles to the outgoing clip's end, the incoming clip's start, and back to roll. At a gap or the end of a track, V selects the one edge. Comma and period trim the selected edit point by one frame left or right. Hold Shift for five frames. The Trim tool ripples one-sided edits. In Select, a one-sided edit leaves a gap when shortened and stops at its neighbour when extended. Each tap is one undo step. A locked partner or insufficient media stops the edit.

Shift+[ and Shift+] trim the start or end of every clip under the playhead on unlocked auto-select tracks. Select leaves the trimmed time as gaps. In Trim, equal trims close time across all unlocked auto-select tracks, cutting clips that cross the removed time as Q and W do. When trims would close different lengths, they ripple on their own tracks only if no other unlocked auto-select track has clips after the first cut. A ripple that would break linked sync is refused. Ctrl+Shift+comma and Ctrl+Shift+period swap one selected clip with the clip meeting it on the left or right. Linked partners move with the swapped clips. Remove transitions touching the swapped clips first. Ctrl+Shift+L turns linked selection on or off, also shown in the toolbar. With it off, clip clicks and marquee selection leave partners out, and keyboard nudges and edit-point trims can act on separate picture and sound.

Ctrl+Alt+[ shows the previous clip version and Ctrl+Alt+] shows the next. Both are free in the owner's Resolve preset. The bracket pair follows the direction of stepping, and keeps Alt+V available for Paste Attributes.

Alt+V pastes the copied clip's transform and keyframes, grade, region, and constant speed. Pasting a different speed resizes the linked clips and ripples following time on unlocked auto-select tracks, as in the inspector. On audio it also pastes gain and effects. Picture and sound can take their attributes from a copied linked pair in one undo step. If either clip uses a speed ramp, the paste keeps the destination speed and tells you.

Shift+R holds the whole video clip on the current frame, keeping its original position, length, fades, and keyframes. Linked sound stays in place. Freeze frame in the clip menu still inserts a silent two-second freeze.

Ctrl+Alt+K opens a sheet of every shortcut by area, generated from this table. Type to search names and keys; Esc closes it. Settings has a button for it too. The key is the one the owner's Resolve preset gives Keyboard Customization, `resolveCustomizedActions`, which is also Resolve's default. The sheet lists the keys and does not change them.

Alt+Shift+S shows or hides the viewer's safe zones and Shift+G its thirds grid. Resolve's Safe Area toggle, `viewSafeAreaToggle`, has no key in the owner's preset and Resolve has no grid action, so both keys are Cutroom's choice: neither is bound in that preset or in Cutroom.

Shift+D shows every graded clip in the viewer as its source until it is pressed again, as Resolve's Bypass Color Grades does with the same key; the Colour section says Bypassed meanwhile. It changes only the viewer: `timeline_view` and exports keep the grade.

Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V is unused in the owner's Resolve preset and avoids the older KDE Klipper shortcut Ctrl+Alt+V. Its only recording-related action, `viewTimelineModeRecord`, has no assigned key.

While typing a note, Enter adds it and Shift+Enter starts a new line. With focus in the note or viewer, Esc discards the draft and Ctrl+Z undoes text by word or after a one-second pause before taking back strokes. If you move to Agent chat or another input, that field keeps its own typing and shortcuts and the note stays open. Ctrl+Shift+Z restores strokes and undone text. Ruler clicks return focus to the note.

Double-click a file in Media, press Enter on a focused one, or choose Open in source viewer from its menu to load it in the viewer. While the viewer shows a source, the playback, playhead and mark keys in this table drive it instead of the timeline: Space, J, K and L play it, the arrows step it, I and O mark the part you want, and Up and Down move between its marks and its ends. Alt+Q switches the viewer between the source and the timeline, as do the Source and Timeline buttons in the tool row; the file stays loaded until it is closed or its path disappears after a file operation. Scrubbing or playing the timeline shows the timeline. F9 inserts the marked part at the timeline's playhead and F10 overwrites with it, whichever the viewer shows; with no marks they place the whole file. After a successful placement the viewer shows the timeline, matching Resolve's default Switch focus to timeline after edit. The file stays loaded. The playhead moves to the end of the new clip, so the next F9 or F10 follows on. Each is one undo step. Marks entered as a source opens take effect immediately, even while its saved marks are still loading. A source's marks are its own, not timeline edits: each file keeps them across reloads and project switches in `.cutroom/view.json`, outside undo history. Marks use source seconds, so changing the timeline frame rate keeps the same part of the file. They use project-relative path, file size and media duration. Touching or restoring identical media keeps its marks. A different size or duration starts unmarked; removing a file leaves its latest saved entry dormant. Only one version is retained per path. Rename moves saved marks to the new path, and undo/redo moves them with the file. Reopening a file starts at its In mark. A still runs five seconds, and its marks set a shorter length. Alt+Q, F9 and F10 are `viewToggleSourceTimeline`, `editInsertOverwriteActionInsert` and `editInsertOverwriteActionOverwrite` in the owner's Resolve preset, where Q itself is Ripple Trim Start.

F11 replaces the clip under the playhead on the enabled destination. It aligns the timeline and source playheads, keeping the original clip's length. There must be enough source on both sides to fill it. F12 places the marked range on a free track above the video destination, creating a track if needed. A source with only sound uses a free audio track beyond the audio destination.

Marks stay queued in the tab during a server interruption and retry after Cutroom reconnects. Closing the tab before a queued value is sent loses it. With two tabs marking one file, the last request to reach the server wins.

Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z step through your own edits. They include Media Rename and Remove and skip imports and outside file changes. Undo in a text field still applies to typing. When later work prevents your undo, the toast names the blocking edit. **Undo both** takes back both edits when that is safe. **Skip** leaves your edit in place and moves the shortcut to the next older edit. Cutroom warns before leaving the page while an undo or redo is still waiting its turn, and one already sent finishes even if the tab closes.

Timeline editing keys Q, W, E, Ctrl+\\, X, Shift+X, Backspace, Delete, Alt+D, M, Cut, Paste, and Paste Insert edit immediately while Source is shown and reveal Timeline in the same press. The timeline panel shows where the edit will land; switching the picture makes its result visible. Resolve 21.1.2 also edits immediately, but leaves Source visible beside its Timeline Viewer. Cutroom shows one picture, so it switches that picture to Timeline. The source file and marks stay loaded. Copy and global Undo/Redo keep their existing behavior. I/O, stepping, and playback still drive Source while it is shown. F9/F10 immediately place its range, then show Timeline, following Resolve's [Switch focus to timeline after edit default](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21.1_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=980).

L and J double the speed on each press, up to 8x; the other direction drops back to 1x, and Shift starts at 4x. As in Resolve, holding K while tapping L or J steps one frame forward or back and plays that frame's sound; holding K with L or J held plays at half speed that way, with sound, until either key is released. The order does not matter: L held first and then K plays half speed at once. K alone still stops. Key repeat never steps twice: a tap becomes a hold after 250 ms. While a voiceover records, K only stops and saves the take. Every shuttle speed plays the timeline's sound: reverse at 1x plays it backwards at full quality, and 2x and faster play short slices at normal pitch. Shift+S, or the waveform button in the tool row, turns Audio scrubbing on or off, as in Resolve. While it is on, dragging the playhead plays the sound under it at the pointer's speed and goes quiet when the pointer rests, and the arrow keys play the frame they land on. It is on by default and the browser remembers it. Shuttle sounds either way. All of this sound follows the source viewer too: while it shows a file, J, K, L, the arrows and its playhead track play that file's own sound, and a file with no sound stays silent. Marks are undoable timeline edits that agents see through MCP. Out is inclusive on screen and exclusive in the engine's range.

The destination keys are the owner's Resolve bindings. Alt+1 to Alt+8 choose or toggle V1 to V8, and Ctrl+Alt+1 to Ctrl+Alt+8 choose or toggle A1 to A8. Clicking a lit destination or pressing its key again turns that kind off. A key for a missing track shows a short notice such as "No A2 yet". The step keys move to the header above or below: up is a higher video track or a lower audio track. Alt+9 toggles picture placement, Ctrl+Alt+9 toggles sound placement, and Alt+0 turns both off if either is on, or both on if neither is. These use the preset's `editTrackDestinationSelectionTargetVideoAll`, `editTrackDestinationSelectionTargetAudioAll` and `timelineTrackDestinationSelectionTargetAudioVideoAll`. Cutroom keeps one destination of each kind and remembers its track while off. The owner's preset has no Add video track or Add audio track binding; both are in a track header's menu. [What the destinations do](editing.md).

Timeline zoom keys hold the playhead still when it is on screen, and the middle of the view otherwise. Shift+Z is `viewZoomSubZoomToFit` in the owner's Resolve preset. It is the only key that fits the timeline; no edit rescales it. Shift+V selects the clips under the playhead on unlocked tracks with auto-select on.

Ctrl+A selects every clip on unlocked tracks, including tracks with auto-select off and disabled clips. It replaces the selection without expanding links onto locked tracks. Shift+A is the preset's Deselect All; Esc also clears selection. Inside a sub-composition, Esc with nothing selected returns to the composition above. Enter opens the selected composition clip. App controls and labels do not select as browser text. Chat, notes, transcript words, code, error messages, export paths, and text fields stay selectable. Copy, cut, and select-all keep their native behavior while browser text is selected; text fields always keep their own shortcuts. A pointer press in the timeline or picture clears the previous browser text selection and returns Ctrl+A/C/X to clip editing. Pointer presses in text controls keep their native selection.

The Y bindings follow the owner's Resolve 21.1.2 preset. Y and Ctrl+Y use unlocked auto-select tracks. Alt+Y and Ctrl+Alt+Y use every unlocked track, including tracks with auto-select off. Each replaces the selection without adding linked clips on excluded tracks. Forward selects clips whose start is at or after the playhead; backward selects clips whose start is at or before it. A clip starting exactly there is included in either direction. A clip spanning the playhead but starting earlier is backward only. These keys select without moving the playhead or changing the timeline.

Q, W, E and Ctrl+\ act on the selected clips under the playhead. When no selected clip is under it, they act on the clips under it on unlocked tracks with auto-select on. A clip contains the playhead from its first frame through the frame before its end, so a cut targets the incoming clip. Q at its first frame changes nothing. W there is refused because it would leave zero frames, and the notice says what to do instead: move into the clip to trim its end, or ripple delete the clip. The same minimum applies to linked partners and MCP trims. Resolve 21.1.2 itself can fall back to a spanning audio bed at a cut and ripple away the remaining picture. Cutroom deliberately keeps the incoming picture target to avoid that fallback. Razor cuts only where the playhead is strictly inside a clip. A selected clip that ends at the playhead stays whole, while auto-select clips spanning that frame, such as a title or music bed, are cut there. Q and W ripple trim one clip and its linked partners: the first of those clips from V1 up, then audio. Every other auto-select track loses the same stretch of time, as in Resolve 20.2 and later: a title or music bed spanning the cut is cut too, so sync holds. Turn auto-select off on a track to leave it alone. Q parks the playhead at the clip's new start, showing the first surviving frame. W keeps it at the cut, showing the following clip. A refused trim leaves the playhead at its original frame unless you have sought elsewhere while it was pending. E and Ctrl+\ both razor. X and Backspace lift the selection and leave a gap; Shift+X and Delete ripple delete it. With nothing selected and both In and Out marked, they take In to Out off every unlocked track with auto-select on instead, cutting the clips across the marks, as Resolve does: I, O, Shift+X cuts a pause out. The marks clear and the playhead parks on the In point, showing the first frame after the cut, or after a lift the start of the gap it left, and one undo puts it all back. The picture is the lowest video track the range cuts, with its linked sound; the header names every other track it cuts, such as B-roll above the picture, a title or a music bed, and says when it removed or shortened a transition. With only one mark set, the header says which one to mark. Ctrl+Shift+X copies the selected clips and ripple deletes them, one undo step, so Ctrl+V or Ctrl+Shift+V puts them back elsewhere; with nothing selected it says so, since copying part of a clip between In and Out is not supported yet. Alt+\ joins two pieces of one clip back into one at a through edit: the selected cut, the cut between two selected pieces, or else every cut at the playhead on unlocked auto-select tracks, so it puts back what E cut, with their linked partners. It refuses with the reason when the second piece does not continue the first's source, when either side was changed, or when keyframes, volume points, a fade or a transition sit there. Ctrl+Alt+' and Ctrl+Alt+; move the playhead to the start of the next and previous gap on tracks with auto-select on: the empty time before a track's first clip or between two clips. Alt+D turns the selected clips off or back on. Alt+Shift+T adds a five-second title at the playhead, lower left, on the first unlocked video track above the video destination that is free for those five seconds, or on a new video track at the top when none is. It never lands on another clip. The title is selected and the inspector opens on its Text field with the placeholder word selected, so type the words and press Enter. One undo removes the title and a track it added. Resolve binds Shift+T to an audio-only transition in the owner's preset, so Cutroom's own action takes Alt as well. A clip that is off stays on the timeline, grey and dimmed, and preview and export leave it out. M adds a marker at the playhead.

Ctrl+T adds a one-second cross dissolve centred on the cut nearest the playhead, between two video, image or audio clips on unlocked auto-select tracks, preferring picture, with the cut's linked sound crossfading with it. With one clip or linked group selected it takes the nearest cut touching the selection. With several selected, it adds a transition at every cut with both clips selected on the same unlocked track, in one undo step. Alt+T applies to picture alone and Shift+T to sound alone. Each cut gets the longest transition that fits up to one second. In a batch, Ctrl+T includes linked partners and keeps existing transitions. The notice counts kept, skipped, and shortened transitions. When adding at a single cut, an existing transition keeps its kind and gets the default length again.

Each press is one undo step. During a burst, pending saves and undo or redo requests run in order. The timeline shows the edit as soon as you press the key, and the clips it moves slide to their new place. A key acts on what was on screen when it was pressed: the same clips, never others an agent's edit moved under the playhead since. If an agent changed those clips first, the edit is taken back and the header says "An agent changed those clips first. Press it again." A key that leaves an agent's clips alone, such as M after an agent's marker, still lands. If the engine refuses an edit in the editor, the header says why, naming tracks, times, and clips as the timeline shows them. The next edit that lands clears the message.

In the timeline, Ctrl+wheel, Alt+wheel, and a trackpad pinch zoom at the cursor, Shift+wheel scrolls through time, and the wheel scrolls tracks. Ctrl-click adds or removes a clip, Shift-click selects through to it, Alt-click leaves linked clips out, and dragging from an empty lane draws a marquee.

On home, the arrow keys, Home, and End move between projects, and Enter opens the selected one. The most recently edited project is selected when home opens. F2 renames the selected project in place: Enter or clicking away saves, Esc keeps the old name. In the editor, Shift+1 opens the project list, matching `fileProjectManager` in the owner's Resolve preset. Ctrl+N adds an empty timeline and opens it, matching `fileNewTimeline`. A browser tab keeps Ctrl+N for a new window, so there use the + button in Compositions; the desktop app passes the key to Cutroom.

## Panels

Resolve's Active Panel Selection keys move the keyboard between panels in one press, from anywhere in the editor: Ctrl+2 Media, Ctrl+3 the viewer on the source, Ctrl+4 the timeline, Ctrl+5 the viewer on the timeline, Ctrl+6 the Library (Resolve's Effects Library) and Ctrl+9 the inspector. They are `viewActiveWindowSelectionMediaPoolClips`, `SourceViewer`, `Timeline`, `TimelineViewer`, `Effects` and `Inspector` in the owner's preset. The preset's Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+7 show the unsupported shortcut notice for bins and the edit index.

In the main timeline, press Shift+A to deselect clips and Ctrl+9 to focus the Inspector. Tab to **Edit project format**. Enter applies the reviewed change in the dialog when **Apply format** is available. Undo and redo include format changes.

- Ctrl+2 focuses the file Media last had focus on, or the first. Up, Down, Home and End then move between files and Enter opens one.
- Ctrl+4 focuses the timeline and shows it in the viewer. The edit, playhead and playback keys act on it, and Tab walks its controls from the zoom buttons.
- Ctrl+3 and Ctrl+5 focus the one viewer, showing the loaded source or the timeline. Tab walks the picture and the transport. With no file loaded, Ctrl+3 says so and changes nothing. Focusing the viewer does not hand the arrows to the picture: that still takes a grab of the selected clip.
- Ctrl+9 opens the inspector if it is closed and focuses it. Tab moves into its fields.

A paper outline shows when the timeline has the keyboard. Other panels mark their top edge. The focused panel is not a Tab stop, and clicking inside a panel focuses what it did before.

Unlike other shortcuts, the panel keys also work while a text field has focus, so Ctrl+4 leaves the agent's message box for the timeline with the draft kept. An inspector field saves its value as it loses focus, as it does on a click elsewhere. Dialogs, menus and popovers keep the keyboard until they close. In a browser tab Ctrl+2 to Ctrl+9 would otherwise switch tabs; Browser shortcuts intercepted before the page receives them still belong to the browser.

## Menus

Right-click a clip, a track header, a file in Media, the picture, or a project on home to open its menu. A project's menu holds Open, Rename, Reveal in folder (desktop app only) and Remove from list. The menu key and Shift+F10 open the same menu from the keyboard: for the selected clips, or for the focused track header, Media file or project. Up and Down move between rows and wrap, Home and End jump, letters search by name, Enter or Space chooses, and Esc closes the menu and returns focus to where it was. A row with a shortcut shows its key and runs the same edit as that key, as one undo step. Unavailable rows use quieter text and stay in the menu. Navigation skips them. In the clip versions menu, End reaches Keep for the current version, and Enter applies it.

A right-click on a clip selects it with its linked partners, unless it is already selected, so the menu acts on the whole selection. A right-click on an empty lane clears the selection. On the picture, with the Select tool, a right-click picks the clip drawn under it and keeps the selection when nothing is. Split at the playhead needs the playhead inside a selected clip. Reveal media moves focus to the clip's file in Media, and Inspector opens or closes the inspector. Add title is in the clip menu, on an empty lane and in every track header's menu, and does what Alt+Shift+T does.

A track header's menu holds its switches (Lock, Auto-select, and Enable on video or Mute, Solo and Arm on audio), Set gain... and Reset gain on audio, the track density, Add title, Add video track, Add audio track, and Remove empty track.

A Media file's menu opens it in the source viewer. Its Insert at playhead and Overwrite at playhead place the whole file on the destination tracks, with no drag. F9 and F10 place the source viewer's marked part the same way, and so do the Insert and Overwrite buttons above the source and the rows of its right-click menu, which also marks in and out and closes it. The playhead cannot go past the last frame, so with the playhead on the timeline's last frame these edits put the clip after it, at the end. In the desktop app, Reveal in folder shows the file, or a project's folder from home, in the file manager.

Rename in a Media file's menu puts the name in a field, selected, with the extension fixed beside it. Enter or leaving the field renames the file, and Esc leaves the name alone. Remove moves the file to the project's `trash` folder, after asking when clips or composition CSS/scripts still use it. [What each does to the project](editing.md#media-files).

Tab reaches the Media list. Once a file has keyboard focus, Up, Down, Home and End move between files; after a click those keys stay with the playhead. Backspace, X, Delete and Shift+X remove the file that has keyboard focus, and the next file takes focus. These are the preset's `editBackspace` and `editDelete`, which remove clips from Resolve's Media Pool as well. After a click on a file they still lift and ripple delete on the timeline. The preset binds no key to renaming, so Rename is in the menu only.

## Mouse

On an audio track, the R button arms the microphone. Only one track is armed. The microphone icon chooses an input, remembered on this machine, and the armed header shows its input level and a Record button. That button or Ctrl+Alt+Shift+V starts playback and recording at the playhead; the shortcut also records with no track armed. In a compact header, Record takes the microphone icon's place while the track is armed. The armed R stays lit, Record turns into a red Stop, a Recording mark appears by the timecode, and a take region grows along the armed lane. Recording stops at the end of the cut or after 30 minutes. Loop is temporarily off. A take uses empty space on the armed track; an overlap goes on a new audio track so previous audio stays intact. One undo removes the take and any track it created. The WAV file stays in `assets/voiceover/` for another take or an agent to use. A dropped microphone block leaves silence at its original position, keeps the rest of the take in sync, and reports the dropped duration. A failed save opens the microphone panel with Retry save, Download take, and a confirmed Discard take. Switching projects or editors is blocked until the take is saved or discarded. Closing or reloading warns before leaving an unsaved take.

A, T and B choose what a press on a clip does, as on Resolve's Edit page. These are the owner's Resolve bindings for `editPointer`, `editTrim` and `editBlade`. Cutroom uses D for Draw and C for Comment to match HyperFrames Studio, as requested by the owner. In Cutroom, Snapping moves to Alt+C and Turn clips on or off moves to Alt+D.

- **Select (A).** Drag a clip's body to move it, along its track or to another. Drag an edge to resize it; nothing else moves.
- **Trim (T).** Drag an edge to ripple trim it. On a cut between two clips, drag to roll the cut. Drag a clip's body to slip it: the clip stays put and shows another part of its source, which moves with the pointer, so dragging right shows earlier frames. Drag its name strip, the top 14 px, to slide it: it moves between its neighbours, the clip before it extends or shortens its end and the clip after it its start, with their linked sound, so nothing else moves. A gap beside the clip shrinks or grows instead. Linked partners slip and slide with it. Each stops where the source or a neighbour runs out, at the next clip, or where a transition on a cut it changes would run out of media. Keyframes and volume points move with the slipped content, as a trim moves them. While you drag, the viewer shows the slipped clip's new In and Out side by side, or the Out of the clip before a slide and the In of the clip after it. A text or title clip has no source to slip; a press on its body selects it.
- **Blade (B).** Click a clip to razor it at that frame.

Click the empty time between two clips, or before a track's first clip, to select that gap, as in Resolve. Delete, Backspace, X or Shift+X closes it: everything after it moves earlier by its length on that track and on every unlocked auto-select track. If a clip on one of those tracks is in the gap's time, or a moving clip's linked partner is on a track that does not ripple, nothing changes and the header names the clip and the track to turn auto-select off or on for. The space after a track's last clip is not a gap.

Linked picture and sound that have drifted apart, after an Alt-drag, an Alt+`,` or a slip of one part alone, show a red badge by the name with how many frames each plays its source late (+) or early (-) against the other, as Resolve does. Ctrl+Alt+L unlinks the selected clips, and links them again. Unlinked clips have no badge.

A transition shows as a box with a diagonal across its cut. Click it to select it alone; the inspector then shows its kind, length and alignment, and Delete or Backspace removes it. Drag either end to change its length about its alignment, up to the longest the media allows; the release is one undo step and Esc cancels.

A press becomes a drag after 3px. Esc cancels a drag and changes nothing else: the tool and the selection stay. Each drag is one undo step. Linked clips move together; hold Alt at the press to move one alone. Dragging to another track carries the clips of the grabbed clip's kind, and their linked partners follow in time on their own tracks. A move onto another clip overwrites the frames it covers. The preview trims or splits the covered clips and hides fully covered clips, including on linked partners' tracks. Locked tracks and picture onto an audio track still refuse the move. A resized or rolled edge stops at its neighbour and at the end of its source. Resolve's Ctrl+Shift drag on Windows/Linux, or Cmd+Shift on macOS, is a shuffle/swap insert, documented on pages 969-970 of the [Resolve 21 manual](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=969). Cutroom does not yet implement shuffle moves.

On the picture, a Select click picks the topmost clip drawn under the pointer, which a thin outline marks before the press. Alt-click steps to the next one beneath, and Ctrl-click adds or removes a clip. Double-click a text clip to type over its words in the inspector's Text field. Drag a clip to move its Position in whole pixels; Shift keeps one axis, and Esc cancels. With snapping on, its edges and centre snap within 8px to the frame's edges and centre lines, the edges and centres of the other clips drawn on the picture, and the safe zones and grid while they show, and holding Ctrl turns that off. Studio uses Alt for this; on the picture Alt-click already steps down the stack, so Cutroom keeps Ctrl, as on the timeline. The arrow keys step the playhead unless the selected clip shows corner handles, which it does after you click it again or drag it. Then they nudge it 1 px, or 10 px with Shift, until a press lands anywhere else.

With snapping on, a dragged edge within 8px of a clip edge, the playhead, a marker, or in or out jumps to it, and a line marks where. Alt+C turns snapping off or on, including during a drag. Holding Ctrl while dragging turns it off until the key is let go; that hold is Cutroom's, not Resolve's.

Drag a file from Media onto a track to add it, with its first frame under the pointer. Dragging the picture, waveform or still out of the source viewer does the same with only its marked part. A normal drop overwrites only the covered frames on the destination track. Hold Ctrl+Shift on Windows/Linux or Cmd+Shift on macOS while dropping to insert and open auto-select tracks. Video with sound also overwrites the same range on its linked picture-sound track; music and voice tracks stay in place. Drag picture above the top video track to create a video track, or audio below the last audio track to create an audio track. Existing clips use those same edge bands when moved. An incompatible edge refuses the drop and explains why in the header. Track creation and the drop undo together. Picture is refused on audio tracks and audio on video tracks. A still runs five seconds.

Media-drop overwrite and insert modifiers follow the [Resolve 21.1 manual, pages 891-893](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/UserManuals/DaVinci_Resolve_21.1_Reference_Manual.pdf#page=891). The owner's Resolve 21.1.2 preset has Insert and Overwrite actions, with no separate mouse-modifier binding. Ctrl/Cmd still bypasses snapping, including during an insert drop.

Shift+8 opens Export, matching `workspacePrimaryWorkspaceDeliver` in the owner's Resolve preset. It has no video-render binding; Ctrl+E exports a Resolve project. When both In and Out exist, Export defaults to that range. Export MP4 has initial focus, including while the destination loads. Enter during loading leaves the dialog open. Press Enter again once ready to start the export. Tab still reaches the fields, Cancel, and Close, and closing restores focus to the opener. Closing the dialog keeps its job running; the Export indicator opens it again.

Drag a clip's volume envelope vertically and the line follows the pointer from the first pixel. The body is a gain scale: +12 dB at the top, 0 dB three pixels above its middle, -6 dB three pixels below its middle, -24 dB near the bottom, and silence at the bottom. Between 0 and -6 dB a pixel is 1 dB at every track height. From -6 to -24 dB, where a music bed sits, a pixel is about 2.3 dB on a default audio track, 6.6 dB on a compact one and 0.8 dB on a tall one. Below -24 dB the line falls quickly to silence. The readout sits on the line at the pointer, in tenths of a dB. The waveform grows and shrinks with the gain and tapers under a fade, but a clip turned far down keeps a third of its height so its beats stay visible. The line is grabbed on itself, within a pixel either side. A press anywhere else on the clip takes the clip, and the middle of the clip is always clear of the line at 0 dB and from -6 dB down, so dragging a clip by its middle to another track moves it. Inside a fade the line still follows the pointer, as far as +12 dB allows. A line within half a pixel of 0 dB lands on it. Hold Shift for 0.1 dB per pixel at any track height; pressing or releasing Shift mid-drag carries on from the gain shown. A mostly horizontal drag that starts on the envelope moves the clip, even after a pixel or two of vertical wobble: the gain shown for those pixels is taken back when the drag passes 3 px sideways. A click released within a quarter of a second that never left a 1 px box changes nothing and selects the clip. Alt at the press still moves a clip without its linked partners. The inspector's Audio field sets an exact value. Shift fine adjustment is Cutroom's mouse convention.

Alt-click or Ctrl-click a clip's gain line to add a volume point there, at the line's level, so the sound does not change until you move it, as Alt-click adds a keyframe on Resolve's volume line. Points show on the line as small diamonds. Drag one to move it: sideways in whole frames, never past its neighbours, and up or down on the same scale as the gain line, landing on the clip's own gain within half a pixel; the readout shows its level and time. Alt-click or Ctrl-click a point to delete it. Adding, moving and deleting a point are one undo step each. Dragging the line between points still sets the clip's gain, which raises or lowers the whole envelope. Alt-drag on the line still moves the clip without its partners. Agents set the same points with [`setClip.automation`](mcp.md#volume-automation).

An audio track's header shows the track's gain in dB beside its name, dimmed at 0 dB. It plays on top of each clip's own gain, as a track fader does in Resolve's mixer, so a bed cut into pieces is set once. Drag the number up or down: the travel reads the same scale as the gain line on that track's clips, so 6 px down from 0 dB is -6 dB at every track height, and Shift is 0.1 dB per pixel. The track changes once, on release, as one undo step, and Esc gives the drag up. Double-click the number, or press Enter or F2 on it, to type a level in dB (`-∞` or anything from -60 dB down is silence); Enter sets it and Esc leaves it. Alt-click, or Reset gain in the header's menu, returns the track to 0 dB. Clip and track gain are independent stages, each supporting up to +12 dB. They can combine above +12 dB. Clips already in an authored audio group are excluded from track gain and show a warning in the inspector. A compact header hides the number; Set gain... in its menu opens numeric entry, and Reset gain names the current level.

In the [mixer](editing.md#mixer), every strip control is reachable with Tab, left to right. A focused fader moves 1 dB with Up and Down, 0.1 dB with Shift, and goes to 0 dB with Home; those keys stay with the fader, so Home still goes to the timeline's start everywhere else. M and S take Space and Enter like every switch. The mixer has no shortcut of its own: the Edit page preset binds none.

With several clips selected, the inspector shows one Gain field for the selected clips that carry sound on unlocked tracks. It reads Mixed when their gains differ. Typing or scrubbing it sets every one of them to that level in one undo step.

A cut inside a fade leaves the rest of that fade as volume automation on the piece. Select the clip and use Inspector > Audio > Clear automation to remove it before drawing a fresh fade. Clearing undoes in one step.

Ctrl+C copies the selected clips with their linked partners. Ctrl+X copies and lifts them, leaving a gap. Ctrl+V pastes at the playhead, overwriting only the copied intervals. Picture and standalone audio stay on their source tracks. Linked sound follows media-drop routing to the overwritten picture's partner track, otherwise a picture-sound track free over its whole span, or a new audio track. Ctrl+Shift+V is Paste Insert: it opens the whole copied span once, including gaps, and ripples unlocked auto-select tracks with the same link and source-cut rules as media insert. The earliest copied start lands on the playhead; relative timing, J/L cuts, clip properties, and source offsets stay intact. Each cut or paste is one undo step; copy adds no history. Copied clips get fresh ids and their own links. Both paste modes select the whole pasted group, including linked partners, so the next edit acts on the copies.

The in-app clipboard survives selection changes and timeline switches within the same project during the server session. It keeps the copied source even after a cut or later source edit. Timelines must use the same frame rate. Other projects have separate clipboards; pasting between projects is unsupported. Copy refuses source-restricted clips by name, as an explicit razor does. Paste Insert refuses a spanning restricted clip with the existing auto-select hint. Alt+V applies the copied clip's transform and its keyframes, grade, region, gain, audio effects and speed wherever the selected destinations support those attributes. It uses the last clip you selected before copying, together with its linked picture or sound. A speed ramp on either side skips speed and shows a notice. If a constant speed change runs out of media, the entire paste is refused. One Undo restores the old attributes.

Drag the divider between the left column and the editor to make room for Media, Agent, Review, or Script. The guide follows the pointer and the panels resize when you release it. Focus the divider and use Left/Right for 16 px steps, Home for minimum, or End for the widest size that fits. Double-click it or press Enter to restore the default. The width is kept in the current window.

Drag the bottom border of a track header to resize that track from 28 to 200 px. Focus the border and use Up/Down for 4 px steps, Home for minimum or End for maximum. The timeline's density menu sets all tracks to Compact, Normal or Tall and clears individual overrides. Compact uses 32 px for both picture and sound. Heights are saved per composition in the project, outside undo history. Escape cancels a height drag.
